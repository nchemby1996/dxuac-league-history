Week 12. Five teams, four chairs, two weeks of music left.

Last week's Matchup of the Week

ESPN had Forrest by 36.1. ESPN does not play.

{{g:clerk_magnifier|DEAN: best possible lineup 146.60. Brooks scored 147.40.}}

Tanner Forrest walked into Week 11 as the No. 1 seed with the biggest projection on the board, 172.2 against James Brooks's 136.1, and then finished 47.6 points short of it. Twiggity Twiggity won 147.40-124.60.

The usual excuse doesn't apply. Antonio Williams did score 23.2 on Forrest's bench, but even the best possible lineup on DEAN's roster comes to 146.60, which loses by 0.80. Tanner Forrest, 0-5 in title games, has found a way to lose a regular-season game with his best possible lineup.

The result moved both of them: Brooks up to the No. 2 seed, Forrest from No. 1 all the way down to No. 5.

Five teams, four chairs

In each of the last three seasons, seeds 1 through 4 got first-round byes, and right now five teams have a real claim on them. Kyle Loy's Etienne the lamb took the No. 1 seed by beating the target, John Barrows, and sits at 8-3 alongside Brooks. Behind them, Ethan Hess, Luke Martin and Forrest are all 7-4, separated only by ESPN's tiebreakers, and the schedule has no intention of letting that stand: Ethan plays Luke this week, and Luke plays Forrest the week after.

Brooks is the hottest team in the league, six straight wins since an 0-2 start. Josh Book is the other one. He lost his first five games, has won five of six since, the last four in a row, and at 5-6 The Dynasty is the No. 8 seed, which in this league counts as a comeback.

At the other end, Nate Hemby has lost four straight and still climbed a seed, to No. 9, because Barrows lost too and fell to No. 10. Jesse Christman has lost six straight and is 2-9. The Golden Shitter goes by postseason finish, so nothing is engraved yet, but Jesse already has two engravings and knows the way.

A first-round pick on the waiver wire

{{g:clerk_alarmed|Puka Nacua: a first-round pick, 180.6 points, unclaimed.}}

The Saturday before his sixth straight win, James Brooks cut his first-round pick. Puka Nacua has 180.6 points this season and sits on injured reserve, and he went out the door for Roman Wilson. Four days later he's still on the wire: a first-rounder that eleven teams have, so far, declined.

A.J. Brown had an even busier week. Nate Hemby, who drafted him in the second round, dropped him; Drew Martin paid $50 to claim him, then dropped him too. Brown has 22.8 points this season and is back where he started the week.

Nate wasn't done dealing. He and Josh Book agreed to swap Jordyn Tyson for Jared Goff, the quarterback who scored 27.36 on Josh's bench in Week 11 while Josh won without him. ESPN showed the deal accepted but not yet processed on Wednesday.

Quick hits

Kyle Loy fixed last week's problem with money. Drake Maye scored 3.02 in Week 10 and Kyle had no other quarterback, so he bid $250 on Cam Ward, who started and scored 28.22 in a 164.12 week, the league's best, that beat John Barrows by 34.12.

Tanner Edwards picked up the Panthers D/ST, started them and got 15, which turned out to be more than his whole margin: Kitties and Samsquanches beat Drew Martin 145.86-132.06. Edwards is 6-5 and the No. 6 seed.

Drew Martin, the Flounder King, made nine adds in Week 11, the most in the league, and The Fumbling Flounders are 3-8 anyway. He also placed the top bid in this week's waivers, $90 for Chris Rodriguez Jr.

Nate Hemby scored 88.40, the low of the week, and lost to Ethan Hess by 34.48 in a series he came in leading 11-1. Only three scores this season have been lower, and the lowest, 85.90, is also his. With 1,308.74 points, the fewest in the league, The Professor is grading on a curve.

Luke Martin beat Tyler Harris 146.64-118.80 and leads the league with 1,801.84 points, 94.40 clear of anyone else, while Tyler slips to 5-6 and the No. 7 seed. Josh Book's fourth straight came against Jesse Christman, 131.36-115.76.

Ethan Hess v. Luke Martin

Why it's the pick: the biggest swing in the seed race on the slate, between the No. 3 and No. 4 seeds, and a series tied 9-9.
