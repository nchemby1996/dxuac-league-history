# Team Trades, Team IR, 2X Champ: The Jesse Christman Experience

No owner in D.J.U.A.C. history has lived at both ends of the record book quite like Jesse Christman. He owns the worst regular season ever and the highest-scoring regular season ever. He has two championships, one of them won with a losing record. He has scored zero points in a week, which is harder than it sounds, and he has taken more commissioner penalty points than anyone. He is, in every sense, a lot.

Welcome to the league, Jesse. In his first ever game, in 2009, he scored 225.5 and lost, because Tyler Harris scored 255.5. Team Christman then won exactly one more game, lost its last nine and finished 1-12, the worst record in league history until Josh Book tied it last season. His draft class was the third-worst ever. Then, a year later, he went 8-5, beat Book 95.5-68.5 and top seed Dennis Hensley 121-94.5, and reached the title game, where Seth Chandler beat him 111-91.5. From 1-12 to the final in one season.

His greatest team never got a ring. In 2012 Team Christman won its first 11 games, part of a 13-game regular-season streak, the second-longest ever, and posted a 125.2 scoring index, the highest single-season mark in league history. It went 11-1, took the No. 1 seed and lost its first playoff game to the No. 9 seed, John Barrows, 155-122. Then he was docked 40 points in each of the next two consolation games and finished eighth.

The rebound years had excellent names. Team Trades (2013) knocked out the No. 2 seed, Book, and reached the semifinals. Team IR (2015) reached the final and lost to Book 127.5-101.5. Then came Password Is Taco. The 2016 team never had a winning record all season, finished 5-7 and squeezed in as the No. 6 seed, yet led the league in points by half a point over Nate Hemby. Jesse then won three straight playoff games, finishing with a 157-134.5 win over Tanner Forrest. Lost a final, won the next one.

Then he named his 2019 team 2X Champ and made it true. He signed Ryan Fitzpatrick as a free agent in Week 16 and started him in the final. Fitzpatrick scored 40, and Jesse beat Luke Martin 161-124. In 2020 he kept the name and went 4-8. That was also the year ESPN showed no starters for him in Week 6, and Book won 143-0.

The low points are low. The 2021 team, officially named --`--`-- --, went 2-11 and drafted the second-worst class in league history, including a kicker, Harrison Butker, at No. 3 overall. Jamaal Charles at No. 5 in 2011 scored 15 points. He has 27 weekly low scores, second-most ever. But the Bear Man era has been steady, and last season Bear's House for Second Chances went 10-3 and took the No. 2 seed before James Brooks knocked him out, 178.82-167.56.

He's also a dealer: 16 trades since 2018, six of them with Forrest, the most of any pair. He has drafted A.J. Green in six different seasons. Book is the nemesis, 17-8 against him in the regular season, though Jesse has won two of their five playoff meetings. Luke Martin is the playoff rival: Jesse leads 3-2 in the winners bracket. And Forrest has never beaten him in the playoffs, 0-3.

## Career at a glance

- **109-101-1** in the regular season over 17 seasons
- **2 titles** (2016, 2019) in **4** championship games
- **8** first-round byes
- **1-12** in 2009 and **11-1** in 2012: the worst and highest-scoring seasons in league history
- **125.2**: his 2012 scoring index, the best ever
- **0**: his 2020 Week 6 score
