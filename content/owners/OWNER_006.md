# Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie: The Dynasty That Never Changed Its Name

Every other owner in this league has rebranded, retooled or wandered off. Josh Book has been Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie for all 19 seasons, and for most of them that name has been printed next to the word "champion." He has won 7 of the league's 19 titles, he is 7-1 in title games, and he is the only owner who has ever won back-to-back championships. He's done that three times. The rest of the league has done it zero times. Tyler Harris even named his 2012 team Joshy Woshy Puddin N Pie, which is the fantasy football version of hanging a rival's poster in your bedroom.

The dynasty started with a heist. In 2007 the No. 3 seed, carrying a 100.4 scoring index and a draft class ranked seventh of eight, beat top-seeded Nate Hemby 797.5-790 in the first title game. In 2011 he did it again, as the No. 3 seed, against top-seeded Nate, this time 328.5-175.5. That 153-point margin is still the largest in championship-game history. A year later he went 5-7, gave up a league-high 1,646 points, and won anyway, beating Luke Martin 165.5-152.5. He was the first champion ever to finish the regular season at .500 or worse.

Then he got greedy. As the No. 1 seed in 2014 he scored 217.5 in the quarterfinals and beat Luke in the final again, 122-81.5. In 2015 his draft class ranked 10th of 10, the worst in the league. He won his last eight regular-season games, went 10-2 and beat Jesse Christman 127.5-101.5 for title No. 5. Draft grades have never meant less.

The first crack came in 2017, when Drew Martin beat him 160.5-137.5 for his only title-game loss. Two years later he drafted Christian McCaffrey third overall and got 442 points, +291 against replacement, the most valuable pick in league history. That team lost in the quarterfinals to Kyle Loy, 123-120, in the luckiest season the record book has ever seen.

The modern titles came via trade. In Week 7 of 2021 he sent Tyreek Hill and Leonard Fournette to John Barrows for Davante Adams and Cordarrelle Patterson, and Adams scored 32 in the final as Josh beat Tanner Forrest 152-133.5. In 2022 he swung a six-player deal with Barrows, went 6-7, slid in as the 8 seed, beat Barrows 188-130.5 in the quarterfinals and won the final 111.5-93.5. No 8 seed had ever won the league before.

And then the fall. In 2023 he went 11-2, took the No. 1 seed and lost his first playoff game 151.5-150.5. In 2024 he finished 12th of 12. Last season was worse: Josh went 1-12, the worst record of his career and tied for the worst in league history, with a -4.28 luck rating, the second-worst ever. He still snuck into the bracket as the 12 seed, because of course he did, and lost his first game to Tanner Edwards, 135.78-128.64. He carries a seven-game losing streak into next season, and the dynasty is three seasons removed from its last ring. By his standards, that's a famine.

His rivals have mostly been his victims. He's 2-0 against Nate in title games and 2-0 against Luke Martin in title games. Harris is his most-played opponent, at 34 games, and Josh leads the regular-season series 18-12. Jesse Christman trails him 17-8 in the regular season.

## Career at a glance

- **138-98** in the regular season, the most wins in league history
- **7 titles** (2007, 2011, 2012, 2014, 2015, 2021, 2022), **7-1** in championship games
- **3** back-to-back repeats; no other owner has one
- **11** first-round byes, tied for the most ever
- **+7.4** career luck, the luckiest in league history
- **1-12** last season, tied for the worst record ever
