# The Professor, the Comeback Kid and the Moose

Nate Hemby founded this league, and the league has spent 19 seasons making sure he never confused that with owning it. He is one of three owners to play every season, and his career reads like a man who keeps winning the regular-season argument and losing the one that matters.

It started gloriously. In 2007, Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay won nine straight, went 11-3 as the No. 1 seed and, in Week 14, dropped 981 on Dylan Plouvier, still the highest raw score in league history. Then he lost the league's first title game to Josh Book, 797.5-790. Two years later the Professor finally collected. Tyler Harris beat him twice in the 2009 regular season; Nate beat him in the final, 148.5-130.5, and the commissioner had a ring.

The next act was even better, right up until it wasn't. Comeback Kid led the league with 1,601.5 points in 2010, finished 8-5 as the No. 2 seed and lost its first playoff game to Harris, the No. 7 seed. But the five straight wins that closed that season kept rolling. Taste Dwayne Bowe opened 2011 at 10-0, and the streak reached 15 consecutive regular-season wins, still the league record. He finished 10-2 as the No. 1 seed and met Book in the final again. Book won 328.5-175.5. Two title games as the top seed, two losses to the same No. 3 seed. Same opponent, same seed, bigger margin.

Then came the lean years: 3-9 in 2012, 5-7 under the extremely honest name I Am Injury in 2013, and a 2-10 last-place finish in 2015. The 2014 rebrand to Moosey Moosey Moosey has now lasted 12 seasons, which makes the moose the longest-serving employee in the commissioner's office.

The moose's finest hour was 2018. Nate opened 0-4. Before Week 5 he sent Rob Gronkowski and Kerryon Johnson to Drew Martin for Calvin Ridley and Marshawn Lynch, then won six straight to grab the No. 4 seed. He survived John Barrows 113.5-108, held top-seeded Book to 88 in a 109-88 semifinal and beat Tanner Forrest 177-139 for title No. 2. Nine years between rings, and a 7-5 team at the end of it.

Since then: no title games in 7 seasons. The best run came in 2023, when the No. 7 seed hung 190.5 on Barrows before Luke Martin ended it 177.5-142.5. The most painful came in 2022, when Nate and James Brooks both scored exactly 154 in the quarterfinals and Brooks advanced on seeding. Last season ended 4-9, a 159.26-79.2 first-round loss to Harris, and 12th place out of 12.

Harris is the rivalry. They've met 35 times, more than any other pair in league history, and they're dead even in the regular season at 14-14. Harris leads 4-3 in the winners bracket. Barrows is the opposite story: Nate is 4-1 against him in playoff games. And then there's Luke Martin, who leads the series 16-5.

The draft file is a highlight reel and a crime scene. Lamar Jackson in the 12th round in 2019 (486 points, +180) is the second-best late-round pick in league history. Laurence Maroney in the second round in 2008 scored 12 points, -223 against replacement, and remains the biggest early-round bust in league history. Nate also took Christian McCaffrey No. 1 overall twice and got 121 points in 2021 and 42 in 2024. He has drafted the Patriots D/ST in six different seasons Loyalty, or a cry for help.

## Career at a glance

- **122-114** in the regular season across 19 seasons, **16-17** in winners-bracket games
- **2 titles** (2009, 2018) in **4** championship games; both losses came against Josh Book
- **7** first-round byes
- **15** straight regular-season wins (2010-2011), the league record
- **30** weekly high scores, the most in league history
- **981**: the highest raw single-week score ever, from 2007
