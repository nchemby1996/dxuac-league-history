# The Sethinator: One Ring, Two Adrian Petersons and a Lot of Fine Print

Seth Chandler arrived in 2009, stayed eight seasons, left after 2016 and took with him something several of the league's lifers still don't have: a championship nobody saw coming.

His debut set the template. Team sethinator was 2-6 after eight weeks, then won its last five and snuck in as the No. 5 seed. Josh Book ended that run in the first round, 168-132.5, but the streak survived the offseason. Seth opened the next year by beating Book 145.5-58, and that made six straight.

That next year was the masterpiece. The league grew to 10 teams, only the top seed got a bye, and Seth went 8-5 as the No. 3 seed with a 113.9 scoring index, the best of his career. Then he walked the tightrope: 118-116 over Brandon Mlekus, 107.5-99 over Tyler Harris and 111-91.5 over Jesse Christman in the final. He'd already beaten Christman 166.5-162 in the game of the regular season. He did all of it with a draft class that ranked ninth of 10. The Sethinator did not need your draft grades.

The ring never came back. Seth reached the winners bracket all eight years and earned three byes, but he went 5-7 in winners-bracket games and never saw another final. In 2011 Nate Hemby flattened him 388-215.5 in a two-week semifinal. In 2012 he started 2-6 again, won his last four for the No. 2 seed, handed Christman the only regular-season loss of his 11-1 year, beat Nate 130.5-96.5 in the quarterfinals and then scored 70 in a 121-70 semifinal loss to Book. In 2014 he went 8-4 by living on the edge, including 88.5-87.5 and 126.5-124 wins over Tanner Forrest, and lost his quarterfinal to Luke Martin by two and a half points.

He also owns one of the great afternoons in league history. In Week 3 of 2015 he scored 250.5 against Luke Martin's 123, the fourth-best weekly score ever once adjusted for era. He finished that season 5-7 and lost his first playoff game to Nate, 109-107. Capable of anything, including losing to the commissioner.

Nate was the problem all along. They met 17 times and Seth won five, and the commissioner took two of their three winners-bracket meetings. Seth was far kinder to Christman and Harris, going 9-5 against each.

The draft table gave him his best and worst moments with the same player. In 2012 he took Adrian Peterson in the third round, No. 23 overall, and got 360 points and +226 VOR during Peterson's 2,000-yard season, one of the most valuable picks in league history. In 2016 he spent the No. 10 overall pick on Peterson again and got 6 points, anchoring a class that ranked last in the league. The record book lists Seth as the second-worst drafter in league history, which makes the 2010 title funnier every year. Add -140 in commissioner penalties across seven games, the fourth-most on record, and this was a man who did things the hard way.

He went out swinging. In the 2016 first round, No. 9 seed Seth beat Josh Book 148-134.5 and ended Book's two-year reign. Tanner Forrest beat him in the quarterfinals, and that was the last of Team Chandler, a franchise whose name went from Team sethinator to Team Chandler Team Chandler to plain Team Chandler, like it was running out of words.

## Career at a glance

- **52-46** in the regular season over eight seasons (2009–2016)
- **1 title (2010)**, won from the No. 3 seed with three playoff wins by a combined 30 points
- **3 byes, 3 semifinals, 1 final**, and a 5-7 winners-bracket record
- **Best season:** 2010, 8-5 with a 113.9 scoring index and the ring
- **250.5:** his 2015 Week 3 score, the fourth-best era-adjusted week in league history
- **5-12:** his record against Nate Hemby
