# The Flounder King: Eleven Byes, One Ring and Half the League's Trades

Drew Martin has had two losing regular seasons in 14 years. He showed up in 2012, went 8-4 as a rookie and took the No. 3 seed, and he has been collecting wins ever since: 103 of them, fourth-most in league history, at a .595 clip that trails only James Brooks. He has earned a first-round bye in 11 of 14 seasons, tied with Josh Book for the most ever.

Then December arrives.

The early teams, USA Mertle's Turtles, set the pattern. In 2012 Book knocked him out 160-150 in his first playoff game. In 2013 he lost to Caleb Hemby, a one-season owner. In 2014 he led the league with 1,679.5 points and a 115.3 scoring index, went 6-6 anyway and lost his semifinal to Book. In 2015 he renamed the team Kansas One and done, earned a bye and lost his first game, which is the kind of prophecy only this league produces. In 2016 Kyle Loy hung 199 on him in the quarterfinals.

Then came 2017 and The Fumbling Flounders, a name he has kept for nine straight seasons. His No. 2 overall pick, David Johnson, scored 8 points all year. Drew started 6-0 anyway, finished 10-2 with a league-best 1,714 points and took the No. 1 seed. The engine was Todd Gurley, his third-round pick at No. 22, worth +204 VOR and still the fourth-most valuable pick in league history. Drew survived Tyler Harris 139-137 in the quarterfinals, buried John Barrows 192.5-111, and with Gurley scoring 49 and 48 in the last two games, beat Josh Book 160.5-137.5. Book had been 5-0 in title games. Drew made it 5-1.

That was the only final of his career. The semifinal has been a wall: he is 1-6 there, and the one win came in 2017. Since the title he has lost five more, twice to Tanner Forrest and once each to Jesse Christman, Book and Luke Martin. In 2019 he led the league with 1,871 points and won no medal. In 2021 he went 10-3 for the No. 1 seed on a +3.23 luck rating, fourth-luckiest season on record, and lost his semifinal to Book 193-139.5. The drought now stands at eight seasons.

Book is the rival. They have met 23 times, and Drew is 7-16, 1-3 in the winners bracket, with the one win being the 2017 final. John Barrows sits on the other end: Drew leads that series 15-5. Kyle Loy has met him five times in the winners bracket and leads 3-2.

Nobody has worked the phones harder. Drew has been part of 22 of the league's 47 trades since 2018, with seven different partners, and the next-busiest owner has 17. Not all of them aged well. In 2018 he sent Calvin Ridley and Marshawn Lynch to Nate Hemby for Rob Gronkowski and Kerryon Johnson, and Nate's title run started that week. In 2025 he sent Jaxon Smith-Njigba, his fourth-round pick and +247.1 VOR, to Barrows for Ladd McConkey and Chase Brown. Barrows won the title.

The draft numbers love him anyway. The record book rates Drew the best drafter in league history, with gems like Devonta Freeman in the 10th round of 2015 (+146 VOR, third-best late-round pick ever). It just keeps sending him first-rounders that vanish: Jamaal Charles (No. 9, 2016), Johnson and Saquon Barkley (No. 3, 2020) combined for 30 points.

## Career at a glance

- **103-70** in the regular season over 14 seasons, a .595 win percentage
- **1 title (2017)**, in his only final
- **11 byes**, tied for the most in league history
- **1-6** in semifinals
- **Best season:** 2017, 10-2 with 1,714 points and the ring
- **22 of 47** trades since 2018 involved Drew
