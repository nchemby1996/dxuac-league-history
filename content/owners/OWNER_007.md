# Mediocre at Best, Champion Twice

For most of league history, John Barrows was the guy whose draft picks you remember and whose seasons you don't. Then he named a team Mediocre at Best and won the whole thing. Five years later he did it again. Nobody in D.J.U.A.C. has had a stranger career arc, and nobody has gotten more out of two title games: he's 2-0 in championships, both against the same man.

The early Johnny Boy years were all draft and no destiny. In 2007 he took Brian Westbrook in the sixth round, +719 against replacement, the most valuable pick of the league's first draft, and beat Curtiss Hemby 899-495 for a 404-point margin. In 2009 he drafted Chris Johnson in the third round and got +238 of value, the second-most valuable pick in league history. As the No. 6 seed he upset Brandon Mlekus 188.5-137 in the first round, then Nate Hemby held him to 104.5 in the semifinals.

After a year away in 2010, things got dark. He finished last at 3-9 in 2011. In 2012 he played spoiler as the No. 9 seed: Dennis Hensley scored 34.5 against him in round one, and then Barrows knocked out 11-1 Jesse Christman 155-122, ending the highest-scoring regular season in league history (by index) in its first playoff game. Then came the basement. Bubbles' Nova Scotian Boys went 1-11 in 2013. Benenellis Boston PD went 1-11 in 2014. Both teams won exactly one game, and both wins came in Week 5. In between, Barrows lost 11 straight regular-season games, still the longest losing streak in league history.

Five seasons as Fernando's GOAT SQUAD steadied things, and then came 2020. Mediocre at Best went 6-6 on a 94.4 scoring index. His No. 2 overall pick, Christian McCaffrey, returned 81 points. But in Week 4 he claimed Justin Jefferson for $400, and the 4 seed went on a run: 177-130 over Josh Book, 166-152 over Luke Martin and 161-140.5 over Tanner Forrest. Title No. 1 came in his 13th season.

The next two years were a cautionary tale about trading with Josh Book. In 2021 Kiss The Ring sent Davante Adams to Book in Week 7, went 4-9 and finished 12th, while Adams helped win Book the title. In 2022 Hall Of Shame scored 2,208.5 points, a 118.9 index that's the second-best season in league history, and took the No. 1 seed. Then Barrows made a six-player deal with Book, including Kenneth Walker III, whom he had claimed for $601. Book beat him 188-130.5 in the quarterfinals with players from that trade in his lineup. The No. 1 seed finished eighth.

Last season, the drafting finally lined up with the winning. Deshaun's RubnTizzuggers built the league's best draft class (2.22, second-best ever) around Bijan Robinson at No. 2 overall, traded for Jaxon Smith-Njigba in Week 5, led the league with 2,169.12 points and went 9-4. Before the final he spent $525 to claim Joe Burrow back. Burrow scored 26.4, Bijan scored 45.9, and Barrows beat Forrest again, 177.1-143.24.

That makes Forrest his favorite opponent: Barrows is 10-5 against him, 2-0 in finals. His least favorite is Drew Martin, who leads their series 15-5. Nate Hemby has beaten him in four of their five playoff meetings.

## Career at a glance

- **97-125-1** in the regular season over 18 seasons
- **2 titles** (2020, 2025), **2-0** in championship games, both over Tanner Forrest
- **8** first-round byes
- **11** straight regular-season losses (2013-2014), the league record
- **+238**: Chris Johnson in 2009, the second-most valuable pick ever
- **118.9**: his 2022 scoring index, second-best in league history, for an eighth-place team
