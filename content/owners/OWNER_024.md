# The Terps: Six Finals, Two Rings and the Best Record That Never Cashed

Luke Martin joined in 2012 and has been the league's most reliable December guest ever since. He has played in six championship games, second only to Josh Book's eight, reached nine semifinals and gone 97-75-1 in the regular season. Every one of his 14 teams has carried the same four-letter tag, TERP, whatever the name on the front said.

The rookie Maryland Terps went 6-6, scored the third-most points and rode a bye into the final, where Book beat them 165.5-152.5. Luke came back angrier. In 2013 he led the league with 1,747 points, drafted the league's best class (Drew Brees in round two, the Broncos Coach in round six) and went 7-5 as the No. 3 seed. Then he beat Seth Chandler, Jesse Christman and Caleb Hemby, the last one 179.5-101, the second-largest margin in title-game history. He was the first owner ever to lose a final and win it all the next year. In 2014 he went back as a No. 7 seed, beat John Barrows 175.5-63.5, edged Seth 119.5-117 and Christman 126.5-120, and then ran into Book again, 122-81.5. Book is 2-0 against him in finals.

Then the Terps fell off a cliff. In 2015 he spent the No. 2 overall pick on Andrew Luck and got 122 points, the second-biggest early-round bust in league history. In 2017 he drafted Luck again, in round nine, and got zero. *CANYOUDIGGIT TERPS* went 2-10 that year, lost their last nine, and the streak reached 10 in the next season's opener, the second-longest losing streak on record. His draft class ranked last. In 2018 he took Le'Veon Bell No. 1 overall, and Bell sat out the entire NFL season.

The second act has been about No. 1 seeds and what happens to them. In 2019 he went 9-3 for the top seed and lost the final to Christman 161-124. In 2020, as *Can you Diggs it*, he won 11 straight, finished 11-1 with a 117.6 scoring index, third-best ever, and lost his semifinal to Barrows. In 2025, *Country Roads Take Mahomes* went 12-1, the best regular-season record in league history, including a 144.5-144.48 win over James Brooks, the narrowest ever. Then the No. 9 seed Tyler Harris beat him 117.40-113.86 in his first playoff game. That's three No. 1 seeds and no title from the top spot.

His titles came from the back door instead. In 2023 a 6-7 Luke won three playoff games and lost the final to Tanner Edwards 180-169.5, with DJ Moore, a player he drafted in six different seasons, scoring 32. A year later *Chig Chig Time* went 7-6, took the No. 7 seed, beat Tanner Forrest, Brooks and Drew Martin, the last one 213.5-123.5, and then beat Harris 186.5-167 for his second ring. Lose a final, win the next one: he has now done it twice.

Plenty of owners have suffered. Luke leads Nate Hemby 16-5, including 3-0 in the winners bracket, and has beaten Forrest 16 times in 24 meetings, the most he has played anyone. The real rival is Christman: 21 games, dead even at 10-10-1, and Christman leads their five winners-bracket meetings 3-2, including the 2019 final.

The draft table has been kind outside the Luck years. Cooper Kupp in the fifth round of 2021, No. 58 overall, returned +271 VOR, the fifth-most valuable pick in league history.

## Career at a glance

- **97-75-1** in the regular season over 14 seasons
- **2 titles (2013, 2024)** in **6 finals**, with 4 runner-up finishes
- **8 byes and 9 semifinals**
- **12-1 in 2025**, the best regular-season record in league history, gone in one playoff game
- **3 No. 1 seeds**, no title from the top spot
- **16-5** against Nate Hemby
