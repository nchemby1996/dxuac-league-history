# Seeds Are for Gardeners: Seth Chandler Wins the Year Nothing Went to Plan

The 2010 regular season was a long, careful argument about who the best teams were. The playoffs ignored all of it.

Start with the newcomer. Dennis Hensley joined a 10-team league and promptly ran it over. Team Mini Denny opened 6-0, stood 10-1 after Week 11 and wrapped up the No. 1 seed. The only blemish in those first 11 weeks came from Seth Chandler, a hint of things to come. Look closer, though, and the 10-1 had some air in it. Hensley ranked only fourth in points, and his luck rating of plus-2.72 wins was the best in the league. Then the bill arrived. He lost his last two regular-season games, scoring 56.5 and 77.5, then lost his semifinal to Jesse Christman and his third-place game to Tyler Harris. A 10-1 team finished fourth on a four-game losing streak.

The best team on paper was Nate Hemby's, and it was named Comeback Kid, which is either prophecy or trolling. Nate lost four straight to sit 3-5, then won his last five to finish 8-5 and grab the No. 2 seed. He led the league in scoring, posted five weekly high scores and drafted the season's best class, the fourth-best in league history, despite his first-round pick, Randy Moss, scoring 69 points. Then No. 7 seed Harris beat him 127.5-115 in the first round. The points leader lost a consolation game to Josh Book by three and finished seventh. That five-game finish did count for something, though: it was the start of a 15-game regular-season winning streak, still the league record, and it ran into next season.

Now the redemption arc nobody saw coming. One year after going 1-12, Christman went 8-5, took the No. 4 seed, held Book to 68.5 points in the first round and then knocked off Hensley to reach the final. From the floor of the league to the title game in one season. He'd eventually win two championships. This wasn't one of them.

Because this was Chandler's year. Team sethinator, the 2-6 team that snuck into the 2009 playoffs, went 8-5 and took the No. 3 seed. In Week 11 it beat Christman 166.5-162, the highest score of the season against the highest losing score of the season. In the first round it nearly went home, surviving No. 6 Brandon Mlekus 118-116. Then it got past Harris 107.5-99 and met Christman again in the final. The rematch was nothing like the original: Chandler 111, Christman 91.5. It was the only title of Chandler's eight D.J.U.A.C. seasons, won with a draft class that ranked ninth of 10 and was rescued mostly by fourth-round receiver Roddy White. Both top seeds were gone after one playoff game each. The No. 1 seed has lost its first playoff game only five times in league history, and this was the first.

Mlekus deserves his own footnote. He took Arian Foster in the 14th round, the best late-round pick in league history, and lost to the champion by two points. Book drafted Brett Favre in the third round and got 77 points. Dylan Flora took Tony Romo in the second, the third-biggest early-round bust the league has seen, then scored 36 points in Week 4, the low of the year. It was his only season. At the bottom, Luten Warrick's THE DRIFTER 24 finished last at 3-10, then won both of its consolation games, a strong finish to a season that was already over.

## By the numbers

- **0-2**: the combined winners-bracket record of the top two seeds, Hensley and Nate
- **2**: points between Mlekus and the eventual champion in the first round, 118-116
- **1,601.5**: Nate's league-leading point total, for a team that finished seventh
- **+2.72**: Hensley's luck rating, the best in the league. It ran out in Week 12.
- **No. 133**: where Mlekus took Arian Foster, the best late-round pick in league history
- **36**: Dylan Flora's Week 4 score, the season low
