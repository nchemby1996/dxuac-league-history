# Sethinator Season: Seth Chandler Wins It All as Both Top Seeds Go Out in Their First Game

The league grew to 10 teams in 2010. Dennis Hensley, Dylan Flora, Luten Warrick and Ryan Long joined, and scoring dipped again to about 106 points per team per week. Seven teams made the playoffs and only the No. 1 seed got a bye. It turned out to be a season where the regular-season standings didn't count for much.

For two months it was Dennis Hensley's show. His Team Mini Denny opened 6-0, stood 10-1 after Week 11, and wrapped up the No. 1 seed at 10-3. Look closer, though, and Hensley ranked only fourth in points (1,449), and his luck rating of plus-2.72 wins was the best in the league. He lost his last two regular-season games, scoring 56.5 and 77.5. The best team on paper was Nate Hemby's, and it fit its name. Comeback Kid stood 3-5 after Week 8, following four straight losses, then won its last five to finish 8-5. It led the league with 1,601.5 points, a 116.2 scoring index and five weekly high scores. Those five wins in a row also started something: the 15-game regular-season winning streak that is still the league record, and it ran into the next season.

Three teams finished 8-5. Hemby took the No. 2 seed, Seth Chandler's Team sethinator the No. 3 and Jesse Christman's Team Christman the No. 4. That was quite a turnaround for Christman, who had gone 1-12 one season earlier. Chandler and Christman also played the game of the year in Week 11: Chandler won 166.5-162, the highest score of the season against the highest losing score of the season.

Then the playoffs flipped everything over. In the first round, No. 7 Tyler Harris and his TANK tyler beat No. 2 Nate Hemby 127.5-115. Christman held Josh Book to 68.5 in a 95.5-68.5 win. Chandler barely survived No. 6 Brandon Mlekus, 118-116. In the semifinals Chandler got past Harris 107.5-99, and Christman beat top seed Hensley 121-94.5. The top two seeds were done after one playoff game each. In the final, the Week 11 rematch was nothing like the original. Chandler won 111-91.5 and took the title. Harris beat Hensley 89-74.5 in the third-place game, so Hensley's 10-1 team ended the year on four straight losses and finished fourth. Hemby, the points leader, also lost a consolation game, 137-134 to Book, and finished seventh.

Chandler won without much help from the draft. His class ranked ninth of 10 by VOR, saved mostly by fourth-round receiver Roddy White (+123). Hemby, meanwhile, drafted the best class of the season. His class index of 2.10 is the fourth-best in league history, built on Peyton Manning in the second round (+109), plus LeSean McCoy in the ninth and Mike Wallace in the 10th. That came despite his first-round pick, Randy Moss, scoring 69 points. The steal of the year belonged to Mlekus, who took Arian Foster in the 14th round (No. 133) and got 294 points (+165 VOR), the best late-round pick in league history. The busts included Book's third-round Brett Favre (77 points, -181 VOR) and Dylan Flora's second-round Tony Romo (122 points, -136 VOR).

At the bottom, Luten Warrick's THE DRIFTER 24 finished the regular season last at 3-10, then won both of its consolation games to finish eighth. Dylan Flora finished 10th, and his Week 4 score of 36 points, in a 102-36 loss to Mlekus, was the lowest of the season. Adjusted for era, it ranks among the five lowest single-week scores in league history. The closest regular-season game was a 113-112.5 win for Harris over Flora in Week 6.

## By the numbers

- **0-2**: combined winners-bracket record of the top two seeds, Dennis Hensley (10-3) and Nate Hemby (8-5)
- **118-116**: Seth Chandler's first-round escape against Brandon Mlekus, on the way to the title
- **1,601.5**: Nate Hemby's league-leading point total, for a team that finished seventh
- **+2.72**: Hensley's luck rating, the best in the league
- **No. 133**: where Mlekus took Arian Foster, the best late-round pick in league history (+165 VOR)
- **36**: Dylan Flora's Week 4 score, the season low
