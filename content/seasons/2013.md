# Thirteen Points Later: Luke Martin Turns Last Year's Heartbreak Into a Coronation

A year earlier, Luke Martin lost the title game by 13 points in his first season. In 2013 he came back with the best team in the league and a record that refused to admit it. Maryland Terps went 7-5 and settled for the No. 3 seed while scoring the most points in the league, the fifth-best scoring index of any season on record. The schedule cost him almost two wins. By December, nobody was counting.

The regular season belonged to a ghost. Tanner Forrest had last been seen in 2008, going 1-9. He came back after four seasons away as It Ertz When Eifert, went 9-3 and took the No. 1 seed. His second-round Peyton Manning scored 463 points, the most valuable pick of the year, and he handed Tyler Harris the season's biggest beating, 176.5-70. Josh Book started 4-0 and settled in at No. 2. Drew Martin posted the year's highest score, 218.5, and did it against Luke Martin. And Caleb Hemby, in his only season in the league, went 7-5 and slipped in at No. 5. Remember him.

The basement had a permanent resident. John Barrows went 1-11. His only win came in week 5. He lost his last seven, the start of an 11-game losing streak that remains the longest in league history. Harris went 3-9 while facing the highest points-against index ever recorded, the league's way of saying everyone saved their best for him. His 2008 title was getting smaller in the mirror.

The commissioner's penalty box stayed busy. Seth Chandler opened the season with a 20-point deduction in each of the first six weeks. Five of them didn't change anything. The one in week 5 turned his 133 into 113 and a win over Drew Martin into a 118-113 loss. Barrows was docked 20 in each of the first three weeks and lost all three anyway, which is about as Barrows as a sentence gets.

Then the bracket turned into an upset factory. No. 7 Jesse Christman, a year removed from the 11-1 team that died in its first playoff game, won two straight, knocking out No. 2 Josh Book in the quarterfinals. No. 9 Harris got a round-one win over Nate Hemby before Forrest sent him home. And Caleb Hemby, who had beaten Forrest in week 12, beat him again in the semifinal, 105-97. The No. 1 seed went out to a one-year guest.

Luke Martin, meanwhile, did what the best team does. He handled Seth Chandler, outscored Christman 161-146 and reached his second straight final.

It was never close. Luke Martin beat Caleb Hemby 179.5-101, with Andy Dalton scoring 35. The 78.5-point margin is the second-largest in title-game history. Martin became the first owner in league history to lose a final and win the next one. Caleb Hemby never played another season. He retired with a runner-up finish and a perfect record against the No. 1 seed.

Martin built the title at the draft table, too: the best class in the league and the fifth-best in league history, with Drew Brees in round two and the Broncos head coach in round six. The worst first-round pick belonged to the defending champion. Book took Doug Martin, his fifth-round hero from 2012, at No. 3 overall and got 61 points. Last year's bargain became this year's bill.

## By the numbers

- **13**: Luke Martin's margin of defeat in the 2012 final. **78.5**: his margin of victory in this one.
- **2-0**: Caleb Hemby against top seed Tanner Forrest, including the semifinal
- **463**: points from Forrest's second-round Peyton Manning, the most valuable pick of the year
- **1-11**: John Barrows' record. The win came in week 5. Hold that thought.
- **61**: points from Josh Book's No. 3 overall pick, Doug Martin
- **6**: straight weeks Seth Chandler opened with a 20-point penalty. **1**: games it cost him.
