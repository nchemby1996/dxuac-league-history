# Redemption, Terps Style: Luke Martin Turns Last Year's Heartbreak Into a 78.5-Point Coronation

A year after losing the title game by 13, Luke Martin came back with the best team in the league. He just didn't have the best record. Maryland Terps went 7-5, good for the No. 3 seed, while leading the league with 1,747 points and a 116.4 scoring index, the fifth-best season index in league history. The schedule cost him about 1.7 wins. By December, nobody cared.

The regular season belonged to a returnee. Tanner Forrest came back after four seasons away, named his team It Ertz When Eifert, went 9-3 and took the No. 1 seed. His draft carried him: Peyton Manning at No. 17 overall produced 463 points and +228 value over replacement during his record-breaking 2013, the most valuable pick of the year. Forrest also delivered the season's biggest beating, a 176.5-70 win over Tyler Harris in week 10. Josh Book started 4-0, finished 9-3 and took the No. 2 seed. Caleb Hemby, in his only season in the league, went 7-5 and earned the No. 5 seed. The highest score of the year went to Drew Martin, who dropped 218.5 on Luke Martin in week 9.

Then there was the basement. John Barrows went 1-11, and his only win came in week 5, 151-88 over Tyler Harris. His 81.7 scoring index ranks among the lowest of any season, and he lost his last seven games, the start of an 11-game losing streak that stands as the longest in league history. Harris had his own misery: 3-9 while allowing 1,729 points, the highest points-against index the league has ever recorded. Seth Chandler played the first six weeks with a -20 penalty each week. Only one of those cost him a game, a week-5 matchup in which his 133 points became 113 in a 118-113 loss to Drew Martin. Barrows was docked 20 in each of weeks 1 through 3, though he lost those games anyway.

The bracket turned into an upset factory. In round one, Tyler Harris (No. 9) beat Nate Hemby 125.5-106.5, and Jesse Christman (No. 7) beat Barrows 158-106.5. Christman kept going in the quarterfinals, knocking out No. 2 Josh Book 144.5-117.5. Caleb Hemby (No. 5) outlasted Drew Martin (No. 4) 175-157.5, Tanner Forrest handled Harris 160-119.5, and Luke Martin beat Seth Chandler 118.5-93.5. The semifinals produced the biggest shock: Caleb Hemby, who had already beaten Forrest 120.5-106 in week 12, did it again, 105-97, sending the top seed home. Luke Martin outscored Christman 161-146 in the other semi.

The title game was never close. Luke Martin beat Caleb Hemby 179.5-101, with Andy Dalton scoring 35 and Eric Decker 31. The 78.5-point margin is the second-largest in championship-game history, behind only 2011. Martin became the first owner in league history to lose a title game and win the championship the following season.

Martin also built the title at the draft table. His class ranked first in the league and fifth-best in league history at 2.03 standard deviations above the season's other classes. It featured Drew Brees in round two (385 points, +150 VOR) and the Broncos head coach in round six, the No. 60 pick, worth +141. The worst pick of the first round belonged to the defending champion: Josh Book took Doug Martin, his fifth-round hero from 2012, at No. 3 overall and got 61 points for it (-79 VOR). Drew Martin's Arian Foster (No. 2, 99 points) and Caleb Hemby's Trent Richardson (No. 8, 114 points) were close behind. Nate Hemby found Antonio Brown in round nine (+111 VOR) and still finished 5-7.

## By the numbers

- **1,747**: Luke Martin's points total, the most in the league, from the No. 3 seed
- **78.5**: the title-game margin, second-largest in league history
- **463**: points from Tanner Forrest's second-round Peyton Manning, the year's most valuable pick (+228 VOR)
- **1,729**: points allowed by Tyler Harris, the highest points-against index ever recorded
- **1-11**: John Barrows' record, with the only win coming in week 5
- **2-0**: Caleb Hemby's record against top seed Tanner Forrest, including the semifinal
