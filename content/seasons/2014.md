# The Empire Strikes Back: Josh Book Drops 217.5 in the Playoffs and Takes Title No. 4

After a one-year interruption, normal service resumed. Josh Book went 9-3, took the No. 1 seed, scored the playoffs' biggest number and beat Luke Martin in the championship game for the second time in three seasons. It was Book's fourth title (2007, 2011, 2012, 2014), and the first time since 2008 that the top seed won it all.

Book's season turned after a 2-2 start. He lost to Seth Chandler 150-117 in week 1 and to Kyle Loy 122.5-109.5 in week 4, then won seven straight from week 5 through week 11. One of those was the year's most lopsided game, a 184-75 demolition of Tyler Harris in week 6. The official record needs one footnote. In week 2, John Barrows outscored Book 130.5-120, but Barrows was carrying a -20 penalty, so the official result was a 120-110.5 win for Book. Tyler Harris took the same deduction in weeks 1 and 2, and it flipped his week-2 game too: his 93 points became 73 in a 83.5-73 loss to Kyle Loy.

Loy was the season's other storyline. Back after five seasons away, he started 4-0 and finished 7-5 with the No. 4 seed. That came on a scoring index of 88.5, second-lowest in the league, and a luck rating of +2.12, one of the luckiest seasons on record. Seth Chandler went 8-4 and took the No. 2 seed by living on the edge: he beat Tanner Forrest 88.5-87.5 and 126.5-124, Luke Martin 122-119.5, and Harris 85.5-82.5. Drew Martin went the other way. He led the league with 1,679.5 points and a 115.3 index, among the best seasons on record, and had only a 6-6 record and the No. 5 seed to show for it.

The bottom was familiar. John Barrows went 1-11 for the second straight year, and once again his only win came in week 5, this time 131.5-129.5 over Nate Hemby. That win ended an 11-game regular-season losing streak dating to week 6 of 2013, still the longest in league history. Barrows then lost his last seven.

The postseason started with a mismatch: No. 7 seed Luke Martin beat Barrows 175.5-63.5 in round one. The 112-point margin is the largest in winners-bracket history since 2011. Tanner Forrest (No. 9) beat Harris 166.5-154 in the other opener. In the quarterfinals, Book scored 217.5 against Forrest's 128.5, the highest winners-bracket score of the 10-team era. Jesse Christman crushed Nate Hemby 166-68, and Drew Martin (No. 5) beat Kyle Loy (No. 4) 156.5-98.5. Luke Martin kept his run going by the slimmest margin of the playoffs, 119.5-117 over No. 2 Seth Chandler. In the semifinals, Book beat Drew Martin 132-106.5 and Luke Martin edged Christman 126.5-120.

In the final, Martin's offense ran out. Book won 122-81.5 behind Jordy Nelson (23), Eddie Lacy (20) and Cam Newton (20). Arian Foster's 16 points were the most any Terps starter produced. It was Martin's second runner-up finish in three seasons, and Book had beaten him in both.

Draft day produced the strangest team-building of the era. Tyler Harris drafted the season's most valuable pick, DeMarco Murray in round three (No. 23, 337 points, +205 VOR), and still posted the worst draft class in league history at 2.61 standard deviations below the field. The reason was his first-round pick: Adrian Peterson at No. 3 overall, who scored 9 points all season (-123 VOR) after missing nearly all of it. Tanner Forrest found Le'Veon Bell at No. 27 (+192), and Seth Chandler took Antonio Brown at No. 29 (+172). Nate Hemby's third-rounder Montee Ball delivered 28 points, and Barrows' second-round quarterback Nick Foles finished 116 below replacement. Luke Martin's class ranked first in the league for the second straight year, which got him to another final but not to a title.

## By the numbers

- **217.5**: Josh Book's quarterfinal score, the highest winners-bracket total of the 10-team era
- **112**: Luke Martin's round-one margin over John Barrows, the largest in winners-bracket history since 2011
- **1,679.5**: Drew Martin's league-leading points total, from a 6-6 record
- **9**: points from Tyler Harris' No. 3 overall pick, Adrian Peterson
- **-2.61**: Harris' draft class rating, the worst in league history
- **11**: John Barrows' record losing streak, which ended in week 5
