# The Empire Strikes Back: Josh Book Takes Title No. 4, and Luke Martin Finishes Second to Him Again

Luke Martin's reign ended the way his first final had: across the field from Josh Book.

Book went 9-3, took the No. 1 seed and won his fourth championship, the first time since 2008 that the top seed won it all. He started 2-2 after losses to Seth Chandler and Kyle Loy, then won seven straight, including the year's most lopsided game, a 184-75 demolition of Tyler Harris.

One of those nine wins comes with an asterisk. In week 2 John Barrows outscored Book 130.5-120. Barrows was also carrying a 20-point commissioner penalty, so the books say Book won 120-110.5. Harris took the same deduction that week, and it flipped his game too: a win over Kyle Loy became an 83.5-73 loss.

Loy will take the gift. Back after five seasons away, he started 4-0, finished 7-5 and got the No. 4 seed with the second-lowest scoring index in the league, one of the luckiest seasons on record. Seth Chandler went 8-4 and took the No. 2 seed by living on the edge, winning four games by three points or fewer. Drew Martin went the other way. He led the league in points and had a 6-6 record to show for it.

And then there was Barrows. For the second straight year he went 1-11, and for the second straight year his only win came in week 5, this time 131.5-129.5 over Nate Hemby. It ended the 11-game losing streak, still the longest in league history. Barrows then lost his last seven again. The only owner who lost to him all season was the commissioner.

The postseason opened with Luke Martin, the No. 7 seed, beating Barrows 175.5-63.5, the largest winners-bracket margin since 2011. In the quarterfinals Book dropped 217.5 on Tanner Forrest, the highest winners-bracket score of the 10-team era. Jesse Christman beat Hemby 166-68, a number the commissioner would like back. Luke Martin got past No. 2 Chandler 119.5-117, which is where Chandler's luck in close games ran out. In the semifinals Book beat Drew Martin and Luke Martin edged Christman 126.5-120.

The final was a mismatch. Book won 122-81.5 behind Jordy Nelson, Eddie Lacy and Cam Newton. Arian Foster's 16 points were the most any Terps starter produced. It was Luke Martin's second runner-up finish in three seasons. Book had beaten him both times.

Draft day produced the era's strangest team-building. Tyler Harris made the most valuable pick of the year, DeMarco Murray in round three, and still built the worst draft class in league history. The reason was his first-rounder: Adrian Peterson at No. 3 overall, two years after Peterson was the best pick of 2012. He scored 9 points. Nate Hemby's third-round Montee Ball delivered 28. Luke Martin had the league's best class for the second straight year, which bought him another final and nothing else.

Four titles for Book, two of them at Luke Martin's expense. Tyler Harris, owner of exactly one title, kept waiting.

## By the numbers

- **4**: Josh Book's championships, two of them won against Luke Martin
- **217.5**: Book's quarterfinal score, the highest winners-bracket total of the 10-team era
- **20**: the penalty that turned John Barrows' week-2 win over Book into a loss
- **5**: the week John Barrows won his only game, two seasons running
- **9**: points from Tyler Harris's No. 3 overall pick, in the worst draft class ever
- **68**: the commissioner's quarterfinal score. Jesse Christman scored 166.
