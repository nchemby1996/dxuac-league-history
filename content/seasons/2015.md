# Worst Draft, Best Team: Josh Book Goes Back-to-Back Again

By the draft-value numbers, Josh Book had the worst class in the league in 2015: 10th of 10, one of the 10 worst classes in league history. He went 10-2, led the league in points and won his fifth championship. Draft grades have never meant less.

Book did hold the No. 1 overall pick, and Adrian Peterson paid it off. After losing two of his first four, to John Barrows and Kyle Loy, Book won his last eight regular-season games, the longest winning streak of his career. Luke Martin and Jesse Christman both went 8-4 and finished two games back.

The best single afternoon of the season belonged to Seth Chandler. In week 3 he scored 250.5 against Luke Martin, the highest regular-season score of the 2012-2016 era and, adjusted for the week's scoring, the fourth-best weekly score in league history. Chandler finished 5-7 anyway. In the same week Tanner Forrest scored 50 against Drew Martin, one of the five worst era-adjusted scores the league has recorded. Seth peaked. Tanner did the opposite.

The commissioner had the worst year of anyone. Nate Hemby started 0-3, won two straight, then lost his last seven to finish 2-10 and dead last, four years after he was the No. 1 seed. He was unlucky, too: one of the unluckiest seasons on record, including a 159-154.5 loss to Tyler Harris in that same week 3. It was also the only season from 2012 to 2016 without a single commissioner penalty. The one year the commissioner docked nobody, he finished last. Correlation is not causation. Probably.

The draft table told the year's other stories. Luke Martin took Andrew Luck at No. 2 overall and got 122 points, the second-biggest early-round bust in league history. Harris spent a third-rounder on Peyton Manning, two years after Manning was the best pick in the league, and got 75 points, the worst value of any pick in the first three rounds. Drew Martin had the league's best class, built on Devonta Freeman in round 10, the third-best late-round pick in league history. Barrows' Antonio Brown, taken ninth overall, was the most valuable pick of the year.

The playoffs opened with the basement fighting back. Because everybody makes it, the No. 10 seed gets a game, and Hemby used his to beat No. 7 Chandler 109-107. No. 9 Barrows beat No. 8 Harris 171.5-140. Both runs died in the quarterfinals, Barrows to Book 126-119 and Hemby to Luke Martin. Christman obliterated Forrest 170.5-88.5, and Loy beat Drew Martin. In the semifinals Book handled Loy 132-95, and Christman knocked out Luke Martin 108-99.

The final went the way 2015 went. Book beat Christman's Team IR 127.5-101.5, and his top scorer was Tim Hightower, with 31. When Tim Hightower is your best player in a title game and you win by 26, the other guy has problems. For Christman it was a second lost final, five years after the first, to go with the 11-1 team that lost its first playoff game in 2012. He was running out of ways to lose.

For Book it was a second back-to-back, four championships in five seasons and five overall. The No. 1 seed won it for the second straight year, and both times the No. 1 seed was Josh Book.

## By the numbers

- **10th of 10**: Book's draft class. **1st of 10**: Book's finish.
- **8**: straight wins to close Book's regular season, the longest streak of his career
- **250.5**: Seth Chandler's week-3 score. **5-7**: Seth Chandler's record.
- **0**: commissioner penalties. **2-10**: the commissioner's record.
- **122**: points from Luke Martin's No. 2 overall pick, Andrew Luck
- **5**: Book's championships, four of them in the last five seasons
