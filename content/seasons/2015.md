# Worst Draft, Best Team: Josh Book Goes Back-to-Back Again

By the draft-value numbers, Josh Book had the worst draft in the league in 2015. His class ranked 10th of 10, 1.81 standard deviations below the field, one of the 10 worst classes in league history. He finished 10-2, led the league in points and won his fifth championship. Draft-day grades have never meant less.

Book did take the No. 1 overall pick, and Adrian Peterson paid it off with 255 points and +122 value over replacement. After losing to John Barrows in week 2 (137-95) and to Kyle Loy in week 4 (134.5-114.5), Book won his last eight regular-season games. That's the longest winning streak of his career and among the longest in league history. He finished with 1,597.5 points, a 110.7 scoring index and three weekly high scores. Luke Martin (8-4) and Jesse Christman (8-4) took the No. 2 and No. 3 seeds, Drew Martin got No. 4 at 7-5, and Kyle Loy (7-5) and Tanner Forrest (6-6) took the last two byes.

The best single afternoon of the 2012-2016 era happened in week 3. Seth Chandler scored 250.5 against Luke Martin's 123, a 127.5-point win; adjusted for the week's scoring, it rates as the fourth-best weekly score in league history. In the same week Tanner Forrest managed 50 points in a 154.5-50 loss to Drew Martin, one of the five lowest era-adjusted scores the league has recorded. Chandler still finished 5-7. At the bottom, Nate Hemby started 0-3, won two straight, then lost his last seven to finish 2-10 in last place. His -2.84 luck rating made it one of the unluckiest seasons on record, and that included a 159-154.5 week-3 loss to Tyler Harris. 2015 was also the only season from 2012 to 2016 without a commissioner penalty: every official score matched the points actually scored.

The draft table told the year's other stories. Luke Martin took Andrew Luck at No. 2 overall and got 122 points (-147 VOR), the second-biggest early-round bust in league history. Tyler Harris spent a third-rounder on Peyton Manning and got 75 points, -194 against replacement, the worst value of any pick in the first three rounds. Drew Martin had the league's best class, built on a 10th-round steal: Devonta Freeman at No. 96, 279 points and +146 VOR, the third-best late-round pick in league history. John Barrows' Antonio Brown, taken ninth overall, was the most valuable pick of the year at +162.

The playoffs opened with the basement fighting back. No. 10 Nate Hemby beat No. 7 Seth Chandler 109-107, and No. 9 John Barrows beat No. 8 Tyler Harris 171.5-140. Both runs ended in the quarterfinals: Book got past Barrows 126-119 and Luke Martin beat Hemby 142-106.5. Jesse Christman obliterated Tanner Forrest 170.5-88.5, and Kyle Loy (No. 5) beat Drew Martin (No. 4) 115-93. In the semifinals, Book handled Loy 132-95, and Christman knocked out Luke Martin 108-99.

The final went Book's way, 127.5-101.5 over Christman's Team IR. The top scorer was an unlikely one: Tim Hightower put up 31 points, Peterson added 19 and Carson Palmer 15. DeAngelo Williams' 29 led Christman. It was Book's second back-to-back title (after 2011-2012), his fourth championship in five seasons, and his fifth overall. The No. 1 seed won the title for the second straight year.

## By the numbers

- **10-2**: Josh Book's record, with an eight-game winning streak to close the regular season
- **10th of 10**: where Book's draft class ranked, one of the 10 worst classes in league history
- **250.5**: Seth Chandler's week-3 score, the highest regular-season total of the 2012-2016 era
- **+146**: Devonta Freeman's value as a 10th-round pick, the third-best late-round pick in league history
- **-194**: the value of Tyler Harris' Peyton Manning pick, the worst of the first three rounds
- **5**: Josh Book's championships through 2015, four of them from 2011 to 2015
