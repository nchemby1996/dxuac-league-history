# Same Name, Sixth Ring

The league grew to 12 teams in 2021. James Brooks and Tanner Edwards joined, the regular season got longer, and the top four seeds got byes. New teams, new format, new schedule. Same ending. *Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie*, under the same name it has carried through every one of Josh Book's titles, won the championship again.

Josh was only the 4-seed, and his season turned on one trade. In Week 7 he sent Tyreek Hill and Leonard Fournette to John Barrows for Davante Adams and Cordarrelle Patterson. Josh limped home after the deal and lost his last two games. It didn't matter. Adams saved his best for the last two weeks. Josh buried top-seeded Drew Martin in the semifinal, the same Drew who beat him in the 2017 final, then beat Tanner Forrest 152-133.5 for the title. That's six championships, the first since 2015, and one title-game loss in seven tries.

The other side of that trade was a horror film. Barrows was the defending champion, his team was called *Kiss The Ring*, and after the deal it went 1-6. Hill and Fournette both put up big numbers for him. The ring did not get kissed.

Tanner Forrest's season is the one that hurts to write. He started 7-0, stretching his regular-season winning streak to ten games going back to 2020. He spent every FAAB dollar he had and led the league in pickups. He dropped 206.5 on Brooks in the semifinal. Then he lost the final again. Tanner is now 0-4 in championship games, three of them in the last four seasons. Always the bridesmaid, now in four different dresses.

The newcomers didn't wait to be introduced. Brooks won his debut by half a point over Nate Hemby, led the league in points and all-play record, posted the season high and did it without spending one FAAB dollar. Welcome to the league, James. Please don't tell the others how cheap it was. Drew, meanwhile, took the No. 1 seed on a scoring index that ranked only fifth, one of the luckiest seasons the league has seen. The semifinal sorted that out.

Then there's the McCaffrey saga. In 2019, Christian McCaffrey was the most valuable pick in league history. In 2020, Barrows took him No. 2 and got 81 points. In 2021, Nate took him No. 1 overall, got 121 points and dropped him in Week 13 for Alexander Mattison on a $420 claim. Three owners, three years, one very expensive lesson. Nate's playoff run ended with a quarterfinal blowout by Brooks. The commissioner gets no special treatment here.

The best pick was Luke Martin's fifth-round Cooper Kupp, one of the most valuable picks in league history. Ethan Hess had the best draft class, with Ja'Marr Chase and a late-round Joe Burrow.

The bottom of the table belonged to Jesse Christman, two years removed from naming himself *2X Champ*. His *--`--`-- --* went 2-11 with one of the lowest scoring indexes ever. His draft class ranks second-worst in league history. He took a kicker, Harrison Butker, No. 3 overall. He spent the biggest bid of the year on D'Ernest Johnson, who gave him one start and then got dropped. 2X Champ, 2-11.

## By the numbers

- **6**: championships for Josh Book (2007, 2011, 2012, 2014, 2015, 2021), all under the same team name
- **83**: Davante Adams's points across Josh's three playoff wins
- **0-4**: Tanner Forrest's record in championship games
- **$0**: FAAB spent by James Brooks, who led the league in points anyway
- **3**: the draft slot Jesse Christman spent on a kicker
- **$700**: Jesse's bid on D'Ernest Johnson, the biggest of the year, for one start
