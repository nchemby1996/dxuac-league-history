# Twelve Teams, Same Old Book: Josh Book Wins Title No. 6 in the League's First 12-Team Season

The league grew to 12 teams in 2021. James Brooks and Tanner Edwards joined, the regular season stretched to 13 weeks, and the top four seeds got byes. Josh Book got the same result he'd had five times before. The 4-seed traded for Davante Adams in October, rode him through December and beat Tanner Forrest 152-133.5 for his sixth championship and his first since 2015.

The newcomers made an immediate impression. Brooks won his debut 181-180.5 over Nate Hemby. He led the league with 2,099.5 points (scoring index 110.1) and the best all-play record (94-49), and he posted the season high, 224 against John Barrows in Week 6. He did all that without spending a single FAAB dollar and with just seven pickups. His second-round pick, Jonathan Taylor (No. 19), scored 389 points (+246 value) and was the second most valuable player in the draft. Tanner Edwards went 8-5 as the 5-seed. Brooks ended up the 3-seed at 8-5. The No. 1 seed went to Drew Martin (10-3), whose 102.2 scoring index was only fifth-best in the league. His +3.23 luck rating ranks fourth all-time. Tanner Forrest started 7-0, which stretched his regular-season winning streak to 10 games going back to 2020 and made it the fourth-longest in league history. He then lost his last three and settled for the No. 2 seed. He spent all $1,000 of his FAAB and led the league with 59 pickups.

The deal that decided the season came in Week 7. Josh sent Tyreek Hill and Leonard Fournette to Barrows for Davante Adams and Cordarrelle Patterson, a player Barrows had claimed in Week 3 for $200. Barrows got 153 points from Hill and 158 from Fournette, but his *Kiss The Ring* went 1-6 after the trade, and the defending champion finished 4-9. Josh went just 4-3 down the stretch and lost his last two. Adams then scored 16, 35 and 32 in the three playoff games and finished with 215 points in nine starts for Josh.

Round 1 sent the bottom eight seeds at each other. Nate beat Barrows 147.5-119, Tyler Harris hung 206.5 on Kyle Loy (162), Ethan Hess beat Luke Martin 197.5-154.5, and Tanner Edwards edged Jesse Christman 157-143.5. The byes held in the quarterfinals. Josh beat Edwards 118.5-96.5, Forrest beat Tyler 137.5-115, Drew beat Ethan 148-124.5, and Brooks routed Nate 155.5-81. In the semifinals, Forrest beat Brooks 206.5-145.5, tied for the highest winners-bracket score of the season. Josh got 38 from Josh Allen, his fourth-round pick (+167), and 35 from Adams to beat the top seed 193-139.5. In the final, Adams and the Patriots Coach scored 32 apiece, and Josh beat Forrest 152-133.5. Josh is now 6-1 in title games. Forrest is 0-4, after 2016, 2018, 2020 and now 2021, and three of those losses came in the last four seasons. Drew beat Brooks 147.5-119 for third.

At the top of the draft, Nate took Christian McCaffrey No. 1 overall and got 121 points (-22 value). He dropped him in Week 13 for Alexander Mattison on a $420 claim. The best pick was Luke's fifth-round Cooper Kupp (No. 58): 457 points, +271 and the fifth most valuable pick in league history. Kyle's eighth-round Tom Brady added +185, while Barrows's third-round J.K. Dobbins scored 0. Ethan had the best draft class, with Ja'Marr Chase in the fifth round and Joe Burrow in the 13th. Chase also scored 64 in a Week 17 consolation game.

The bottom of the table belonged to Jesse. His *--`--`-- --* went 2-11 with a 78.7 scoring index, the fourth-lowest ever, plus a 22-120 all-play record and five weekly low scores. His draft class ranks second-worst in league history. He took kicker Harrison Butker No. 3 overall (+8) and Calvin Ridley in the second round (71 points, -115). Jesse's $700 bid on D'Ernest Johnson in Week 7 was the biggest of the year, and Johnson gave him one start and 26 points before being dropped in Week 9. The season's low came in Week 9, when Forrest beat Jesse 84-62.
