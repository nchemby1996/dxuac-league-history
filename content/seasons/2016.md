# Password Is Taco: Jesse Christman Goes 5-7 and Wins Everything

Four years earlier, Jesse Christman went 11-1, lost his first playoff game and watched Josh Book win the title at 5-7. In 2016 he took the other side of that deal.

Christman never had a winning record all season. He opened with a loss, reached .500 three times, never got above it and finished 5-7. Five teams finished 5-7, and they were seeded by points. Christman had the most in the league, half a point ahead of Nate Hemby, which made him the No. 6 seed and gave him the last bye. The record undersold Password Is Taco badly. In week 2 he beat John Barrows 212.5-72, the largest win of the 2012-2016 era and, adjusted for the week, the third-best weekly score in league history. He closed the regular season by beating the No. 1 seed, Tanner Forrest, 176.5-126.5. Consider it a preview.

Forrest earned that top seed at 8-4 on the back of David Johnson, taken eighth overall and the third-most valuable pick in league history. The same draft gave him Keenan Allen in round three, who scored 9 points. Luke Martin took the No. 2 seed with the league's best draft class, his third in four years. Hemby went 8-4 for the No. 3 seed behind Ezekiel Elliott and won the closest game of the year, 102.5-102 over Seth Chandler.

Adrian Peterson, meanwhile, continued his tour of the league's draft boards. Best pick of 2012 for Chandler. Nine points for Tyler Harris in 2014. Taken No. 1 overall by a champion in 2015. In 2016 Chandler took him again, 10th overall, and got 6 points. Drew Martin held picks No. 1 and No. 9 and got 11 points from the second one, Jamaal Charles.

The defending champion faded. Book lost his last three regular-season games, the last of them to Barrows, and slipped to 5-7 and the No. 8 seed. Barrows lost seven of his first eight, won his last four and still finished as the No. 10 seed with the fewest points in the league. The commissioner handed out penalties early and often: Kyle Loy was docked 20 in each of the first seven weeks, Barrows in each of the first four and Chandler once. Not one result changed. Loy then dropped 199 on Drew Martin in the quarterfinals, the highest score of the playoffs, as if to make a point.

Book's two-year reign ended in round one, when No. 9 Chandler beat him 148-134.5 in what turned out to be Chandler's final season. Harris won his opener and went out to Luke Martin in the quarterfinals, eight years removed from his only title.

Then Christman went to work. He beat No. 3 Hemby 118.5-92. He beat No. 2 Luke Martin 144-117. And in the final he beat No. 1 Forrest again, 157-134.5, his best playoff total of the year, with the Cowboys head coach scoring 27. Three, two, one, liftoff.

It was Christman's third title game and his first win, after losing the 2010 final and the 2015 final. He became the second owner, after Luke Martin in 2013, to lose a final and win the next one, and the second champion, after Book in 2012, without a winning record. Seven years after going 1-12, Jesse Christman finally had his ring.

For Forrest it was the first runner-up finish of his career. It would not be the last.

## By the numbers

- **5-7**: Christman's record, the second champion without a winning record
- **3-2-1**: the seeds Christman beat on the way to the title, in that order
- **0.5**: the gap between Christman and Hemby for the league's points title
- **7**: straight weeks Kyle Loy opened with a 20-point penalty. **0**: results it changed.
- **6**: points from Seth Chandler's No. 10 overall pick, Adrian Peterson
- **9**: points from Tanner Forrest's third-round Keenan Allen
