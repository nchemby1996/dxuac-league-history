# Password Is Taco: Jesse Christman Never Had a Winning Record, Then Won Everything

Jesse Christman never had a winning record in the 2016 regular season. He opened with a loss, reached .500 three times and never got above it, and finished 5-7. Then he beat the No. 3, No. 2 and No. 1 seeds in consecutive weeks and won the championship. Four years after his 11-1 team went out in its first playoff game, Christman won his first title with a team that barely made the bye line.

The record undersold Password Is Taco. Christman led the league with 1,544.5 points, half a point ahead of Nate Hemby's 1,544, and posted three weekly highs. In week 2 he beat John Barrows 212.5-72, a 140.5-point official margin and the largest win of the 2012-2016 era. Adjusted for the week's scoring, the 212.5 ranks as the third-best weekly score in league history. Christman was also unlucky, with a luck rating of -1.55. Five teams finished 5-7, and the seeds among them ran in points order, which put Christman at No. 6 with the last bye. He needed his week-12 game to get there and won it 176.5-126.5 over the No. 1 seed, Tanner Forrest. It was a preview of the final.

Forrest earned that top seed at 8-4 on the strength of the season's most valuable pick: David Johnson at No. 8 overall, 361 points and +226 VOR, the third-most valuable pick in league history. The same draft also gave Forrest the worst pick of the first three rounds: Keenan Allen at No. 28, 9 points and -143 VOR. Luke Martin (7-5) took the No. 2 seed with the league's best draft class, led by Aaron Rodgers' 407 points and a 13th-round LeGarrette Blount (+103 VOR). Nate Hemby went 8-4 for the No. 3 seed behind second-rounder Ezekiel Elliott (+178 VOR). Hemby also won the season's closest game, 102.5-102 over Seth Chandler in week 8, one of the narrowest wins in league history. Drew Martin held two first-round picks, No. 1 and No. 9. Julio Jones delivered 208 points; Jamaal Charles delivered 11. Seth Chandler's No. 10 pick, Adrian Peterson, managed 6, part of the league's worst class in Chandler's final season.

The defending champion faded. Josh Book lost his last three regular-season games, the last a 127.5-100.5 loss to John Barrows, and slipped to 5-7 and the No. 8 seed. Barrows lost seven of his first eight, then won his last four to reach 5-7. With the league's fewest points, he was still the No. 10 seed. Commissioner penalties were everywhere early but changed nothing. Kyle Loy was docked 20 in each of the first seven weeks, Barrows in weeks 1 through 4 and Chandler in week 1, and no result flipped.

Book's two-year reign ended in round one, when No. 9 Seth Chandler beat him 148-134.5. Tyler Harris (No. 7) beat Barrows 169-113. In the quarterfinals, Christman beat Hemby 118.5-92, Forrest got past Chandler 110-91, Luke Martin beat Harris 106.5-78.5, and No. 5 Kyle Loy dropped 199 on No. 4 Drew Martin (114.5). Le'Veon Bell's 51 points that week were the season's top individual game in the lineup data. In the semifinals, Forrest beat Loy 139.5-122.5, and Christman took out Luke Martin 144-117.

The final was Christman's best playoff total. He beat Forrest 157-134.5, with the Cowboys head coach scoring 27, LeSean McCoy 25, and Odell Beckham Jr. and Isaiah Crowell 21 apiece. Doug Baldwin (33) and David Johnson (30) kept Forrest in it. It was Christman's third title-game appearance and his first win, a year after losing the 2015 final to Josh Book. For Forrest, it was the first runner-up finish of his career.

## By the numbers

- **5-7**: Jesse Christman's regular-season record; he never had a winning record all season
- **3-2-1**: the seeds Christman beat on the way to the title, in that order
- **0.5**: the gap between Christman (1,544.5) and Nate Hemby (1,544) for the league's points title
- **140.5**: Christman's week-2 margin over John Barrows, the largest win of the 2012-2016 era
- **+226**: David Johnson's value over replacement, the third-most valuable pick in league history
- **199**: Kyle Loy's quarterfinal score, the highest of the 2016 playoffs
