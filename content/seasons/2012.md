# Eleven and One and Done: Jesse Christman Builds the Greatest Regular Season Ever, Josh Book Wins It at 5-7

Three years earlier, Jesse Christman went 1-12 and set the league's standard for misery. In 2012 he set the other one.

Team Christman won its first 11 games. It led the league in scoring by 224 points and posted the highest season scoring index in league history, a quarter better than the average team. Counting the end of 2011, the winning streak reached 13. The only loss came in week 12, when Seth Chandler hung 162.5 on him, and nobody worried about it. Christman had the No. 1 seed, a bye and the look of a team that would be remembered.

He would be remembered. Just not like that.

Josh Book, the defending champion, spent the fall on the wrong end of the schedule. He scored the second-most points in the league, gave up the most, and finished more than two wins behind what his scoring deserved. A week-12 win over Luke Martin got him to 5-7 and the No. 6 seed, the last bye. In a league where all ten teams make the playoffs, that counts as a plan.

Brandon Mlekus lived on the other side of the luck ledger. He scored the fewest points in the league and still went 6-6, the second-luckiest season on record. It should have been better. Mlekus carried a 20-point commissioner penalty in each of the first four weeks, and two of them turned wins into losses. On points actually scored, he went 8-4. The beneficiaries were Seth Chandler, who finished 6-6 and took the No. 2 seed, and the commissioner himself. Nate Hemby lost to Mlekus on the field in week 3 and won in the books. It was one of his three wins. A year after going 10-2 as the No. 1 seed, the commissioner was the No. 10 seed.

Tyler Harris spent the season playing under the name Joshy Woshy Puddin N Pie. It did not help. He finished last.

The playoffs opened with Dennis Hensley scoring 34.5, the lowest total ever recorded in a winners-bracket game. Then came the quarterfinals, and John Barrows. The No. 9 seed put up 155. The 11-1 juggernaut managed 122. The greatest regular season in league history lasted exactly one playoff game.

It got worse. Christman was docked 40 points in each of his two consolation games, and the second deduction turned a win over Hensley into a 117.5-114 loss. The best team the league had ever seen finished eighth.

Book, meanwhile, kept walking through the door Barrows had opened. He survived Drew Martin by 10, flattened Chandler 121-70 in the semifinal and met Luke Martin, a first-year owner who had finished 6-6, in the final. Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie won 165.5-152.5, with Jamaal Charles scoring 33. That made it back-to-back titles, and Book became the first champion in league history without a winning record.

The 11-1 team was out after one playoff game. The 5-7 team took the trophy. Some years the league explains itself. This was not one of them.

The draft had its own verdicts. Seth Chandler took Adrian Peterson in the third round and got the most valuable pick of the year. Book's title ran through a fifth-rounder, Doug Martin, whose 69-point week 9 was the biggest single game in the season's lineup data. And Tyler Harris spent the No. 9 overall pick on Michael Vick, who finished 127 points below replacement. Harris, at least, was consistent.

## By the numbers

- **11-1**: Jesse Christman's regular season. **0-1**: his winners-bracket record.
- **34.5**: Dennis Hensley's round-one score, the lowest in winners-bracket history
- **5-7**: Josh Book's record, the first champion without a winning record
- **8-4**: Brandon Mlekus on points actually scored. **6-6**: Mlekus after the penalties.
- **3**: Nate Hemby's wins, one of them handed over by a penalty
- **-127**: Michael Vick's value over replacement as a No. 9 overall pick
