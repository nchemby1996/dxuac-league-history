# 11-1 and Gone in One: Josh Book Wins It All at 5-7 While the Greatest Regular Season Ever Dies in the Quarterfinals

For eleven weeks, 2012 belonged to Jesse Christman. Team Christman won its first 11 games, scored 1,792.5 points (the most in the league by 224 over the next-best team), took four weekly high scores and finished with a scoring index of 125.2: 25% above the league average, the highest single-season mark in league history. Counting the end of 2011, the streak reached 13 straight regular-season wins, the second-longest the league has ever seen. The only blemish came in week 12, when Seth Chandler's Team sethinator hung 162.5 on him in a 162.5-139 win. That loss looked harmless at the time. Christman had the No. 1 seed and a bye. It was the last good news he got all year.

The rest of the league spent the fall in a traffic jam. Drew Martin went 8-4 in his first season and took the No. 3 seed; fellow newcomer Luke Martin went 6-6 but scored the third-most points. Seth Chandler started 2-6, then won his last four to reach 6-6 and grab the No. 2 seed. And Josh Book, the defending champion, went 5-7 despite scoring the second-most points in the league (1,568.5). His problem was the schedule: he gave up 1,646 points, the most in the league and the second-highest points-against index in league history, and his record sat 2.39 wins below what his all-play numbers deserved. A week-12 win over Luke Martin, 146-121.5, got him to 5-7 and the No. 6 seed, the last of the six byes. On the other side of the luck ledger, Brandon Mlekus scored the fewest points in the league (1,115.5) and still went 6-6, a luck rating of +3.55 that ranks as the second-luckiest season on record. Mlekus also carried -20 commissioner penalties in weeks 1 through 4, and two of them flipped results: a 93.5-85.5 win over Seth Chandler in week 2 went into the books as a 73.5-85.5 loss, and a 110-103 win over Nate Hemby in week 3 became a 90-103 loss.

The playoffs opened with a historic faceplant. In round one, Dennis Hensley posted 34.5 points against John Barrows (111.5), the lowest score ever recorded in a winners-bracket game. Nate Hemby, the No. 10 seed, beat Tyler Harris 139.5-120. Then came the quarterfinals, and the moment that defines the season: No. 9 seed John Barrows put up 155 and knocked out the 11-1 Christman, who managed 122. Elsewhere, Seth Chandler beat Nate Hemby 130.5-96.5, Luke Martin beat Mlekus 136-91.5, and Josh Book survived Drew Martin 160-150. In the semifinals, Book flattened Chandler 121-70 and Luke Martin ended Barrows' run 147-114.

The title game was a shootout. Josh Book beat Luke Martin 165.5-152.5, with Jamaal Charles leading the way at 33 points while Aaron Rodgers' 36 kept the Terps close. It was Book's second straight championship (2011 and 2012), and he became the first champion in league history to finish the regular season at .500 or worse.

Christman's postseason only got worse. In the consolation games he was docked 40 points in each of weeks 15 and 16. The week-16 deduction cost him a game: his 154 points became 114 in a 117.5-114 loss to Dennis Hensley. The greatest regular season in league history ended in eighth place.

At the draft, Seth Chandler found the year's best value in round three: Adrian Peterson at No. 23 overall, 360 points and +226 over replacement during Peterson's 2,000-yard season. Josh Book's title ran through a fifth-rounder, rookie Doug Martin (No. 48, +168 VOR), whose 69-point week 9 was the biggest single-player game in the season's lineup data. The busts came early: Tyler Harris spent the No. 9 pick on Michael Vick (115 points, -127 VOR), and Mlekus took Maurice Jones-Drew at No. 10 (70 points). At the bottom, Nate Hemby went 3-9 as the No. 10 seed despite scoring more than four other teams. His -2.5 luck rating made it one of the unluckiest seasons on record.

## By the numbers

- **125.2**: Jesse Christman's scoring index, the highest single season in league history
- **34.5**: Dennis Hensley's round-one total, the lowest score ever in a winners-bracket game
- **5-7**: Josh Book's regular-season record, the first champion at .500 or worse
- **1,646**: points scored against Book, the most in the league
- **+226**: Adrian Peterson's value over replacement as a third-round pick, the best of the draft
- **2**: results flipped by Brandon Mlekus' early-season penalties
