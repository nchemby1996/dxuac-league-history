# The Professor Finally Collects

Every league has the guy who's great until it counts. In 2009, that guy was Tyler Harris.

He opened the season by hanging 255.5 on Jesse Christman, the biggest number anyone would post all year, and never really looked back. He took sole possession of first place in Week 5 and kept it, led the league in scoring, and beat Nate Hemby both times they met. By December, tdogg beast had the look of a team that would be remembered. One year after his title, Harris looked ready for another.

Nate, meanwhile, started 0-2 and opened with 123 points, the lowest score in the league that week, the kind of number that gets you laughed out of most group chats. Then he quietly figured it out, climbed to 8-5 and snuck into the No. 2 seed. Nobody was scared of him. Harris had already beaten him twice.

The playoffs were a formality until they weren't. Harris, rested by a first-round bye, dropped 210.5 on Josh Book in the semifinal, the kind of number that ends arguments. Then he met Nate in the final and scored his lowest playoff total of the year at the worst possible moment. Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay won 148.5-130.5. Two years after losing the league's first title game by seven and a half points, the Professor had his ring. Harris had a great regular season and a front-row seat for somebody else's party. He hasn't won a title since.

If Harris was the year's tragedy, Jesse Christman was its horror film. In his first ever D.J.U.A.C. game he scored 225.5, a total that wins almost any week in almost any season. He lost, because it was Harris's week to score 255.5. It set the tone. Jesse won exactly one game all year, in Week 4, then lost his final nine, finishing 1-12. Nobody in league history has finished worse, and the only owner to match it is Josh Book, of all people, in 2025. Christman also posted the season's lowest score, 61, and his draft class ranked dead last. Welcome to the league, Jesse.

Elsewhere, Seth Chandler sat at 2-6, rattled off five straight wins and stole a playoff spot, one of four 7-6 teams that filled seeds three through six. Hold onto that name. Next season it's on the trophy. Noah Monsour scored the third-most points in the league, finished 5-8 and missed the playoffs anyway, the unluckiest team in the league in his only D.J.U.A.C. season. And John Barrows drafted Chris Johnson in the third round, then watched him run for 2,000 yards. It still ranks as the second-most valuable pick in league history. It got Barrows a first-round playoff win over Brandon Mlekus and nothing more. Book, whose draft class ranked first, beat him for third.

## By the numbers

- **148.5-130.5**: the title game, and Nate's first win over Harris in three tries that season
- **1-12**: Jesse Christman's record. The league floor, shared only with a 2025 Josh Book.
- **255.5**: Harris's Week 1 score, which made Christman's 225.5 a loss
- **4**: teams that finished 7-6 and took seeds three through six
- **2,018**: points scored by Noah Monsour, third in the league, for a team that watched the playoffs from the outside
- **+238**: value over replacement for Barrows' Chris Johnson, the second-most valuable pick in league history
