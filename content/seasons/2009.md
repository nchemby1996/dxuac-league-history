# The Professor Gets His Ring: Nate Hemby Beats the Team He Couldn't Beat All Year

The league dropped back to eight teams in 2009 and brought in new blood: Seth Chandler, Noah Monsour and Jesse Christman joined Brandon Mlekus, who was back for a second season. Scores held steady at about 150 points per team per week. Six teams would make the playoffs, and the top two would get byes.

From the opening weekend this looked like Tyler Harris's year. His tdogg beast scored 255.5 in Week 1, the highest score of the season, and was alone in first place from Week 5 on. He finished 10-3 with a league-high 2,226.5 points and a scoring index of 114.2. Nate Hemby, meanwhile, opened with 123 points, the lowest score in the league that week, and started 0-2. He recovered to go 8-5, finish second in points with 2,072, and take the No. 2 seed. The two met twice in the regular season and Harris won both, 173.5-164.5 in Week 6 and 171.5-145 in Week 11.

Below them it was a traffic jam. Four teams finished 7-6, and Brandon Mlekus, Josh Book, Seth Chandler and John Barrows filled seeds three through six. Chandler got in the hard way. His Team sethinator was 2-6 after eight weeks and won its last five. The team left out was Noah Monsour's little boy pink. Monsour scored 2,018 points, third-most in the league, and finished 5-8. His luck rating of minus-2.28 wins was the worst in the league.

The first round split. Book beat Chandler 168-132.5. Barrows, the No. 6 seed, beat No. 3 Mlekus 188.5-137. The semifinals then went to form. Nate Hemby held Barrows to 104.5 in a 152.5-104.5 win, and Harris ran past Book 210.5-152.5. That set up the matchup the regular season had settled twice in Harris's favor. This time it went the other way: Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay 148.5, tdogg beast 130.5. Two years after losing the first title game by 7.5 points, Nate Hemby was a champion. Book took third with a 152.5-136 win over Barrows.

Then there was Jesse Christman. His Team Christman scored 225.5 in Week 1, the third-highest score of the season, and lost to Harris. Adjusted for era, it's still the third-highest-scoring loss in league history. It was all downhill from there. Christman won once, in Week 4, then lost his last nine. His 1-12 record (.077) is the worst regular season in league history. He finished with a 77.3 scoring index and the season's lowest score, 61 in Week 12 against Barrows. His draft class ranked eighth of eight, and at -2.27 it's the third-worst class in league history.

The draft's best pick belonged to Barrows: Chris Johnson in the third round (No. 23), 399 points and +238 VOR. That was Johnson's 2,000-yard season, and the pick still ranks as the second-most valuable in league history. Barrows also hit on DeSean Jackson in the first round (+146) and Ray Rice in the eighth (+101). Book found Aaron Rodgers in the third round (412 points) and Vernon Davis in the 18th (270 points), and his class ranked first. The new champion's draft was solid but not spectacular. Nate Hemby's class ranked third. His first-rounder, Matt Forte, came in right at replacement level (-1 VOR), and the best pick was second-round Randy Moss at +119. The costliest early miss was Mlekus' second-round quarterback Matt Ryan, at -93 VOR.

## By the numbers

- **148.5-130.5**: the title game, Nate Hemby's first win over Tyler Harris in three tries that season
- **1-12**: Jesse Christman's record, the worst regular season in league history (.077)
- **255.5**: Harris's Week 1 score, the season high, against Christman's 225.5
- **4**: teams that finished 7-6 and took seeds three through six
- **2,018**: points scored by Noah Monsour, third in the league, for a 5-8 team that missed the playoffs
- **+238**: VOR for John Barrows' third-round pick Chris Johnson, the second-most valuable pick in league history
