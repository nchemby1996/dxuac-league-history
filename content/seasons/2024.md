# Chig Chig Time: Luke Martin Cashes the Rebound

Twelve months after losing the final by 10.5 points, Luke Martin came back and won the thing. In November, it would have been a terrible bet.

Chig Chig Time lost its opener to Kyle Loy by one point, won four straight, then fell apart: 1-5 over the next six weeks, including a 90.5-point beating from Tanner Edwards. At 5-6 with two weeks left, Luke was a spectator with a roster. He won both games, finished 7-6 and slipped in as the 7 seed with no weekly high scores. Nobody circled him.

Everybody circled Tyler Harris. April Showers Bring Zay Flowers started 0-2, then went 9-2 the rest of the way, led the league in points and took the No. 1 seed. Harris also had the best draft in the league: Ja'Marr Chase at No. 5 was the most valuable pick of the year, and Brian Thomas Jr. in round 10 was a steal. It had the look of the year the drought finally ended. Harris hadn't won a title since 2008.

The trade market ran on Saquon Barkley. Josh Book drafted him ninth overall, then shipped him to Jesse Christman in an eight-player deal in week 9. Saquon gave Christman 133 points in four weeks. Christman then flipped him to James Brooks for Bijan Robinson and Courtland Sutton. Three teams rostered the same running back in one season, and the guy who drafted him finished 12th, the worst season of Book's career. Two years after his seventh ring, Joshy Woshy lost a first-round game to Nate Hemby.

The hard-luck award went to Tanner Forrest. DEAN scored the second-most points in the league and still went 5-8, one of the five unluckiest seasons on record. In week 14 Forrest made the largest winning FAAB bid in league history, $745 of his $1,000 on Isaac Guerendo. Guerendo scored 26, and Forrest lost to Luke Martin anyway. Somewhere a budget spreadsheet wept.

Then the playoffs got loud. Loy, the 11 seed, put up 247 on Edwards, the defending champion, in the first round. It's the highest winners-bracket score since at least 2012, and it sent the champ home in a week. Luke Martin, meanwhile, just kept winning: Forrest, then Brooks 151.5-105, then a 213.5-123.5 semifinal demolition of Drew Martin behind Jonathan Taylor and Jayden Daniels. Harris edged Christman 123-118.5 on the other side and the final was set: the 1 seed against the 7.

Daniels scored 38. The Eagles Coach scored 27.5. Luke Martin won 186.5-167. That's his second title, and the second time he's lost a final and won the next one, after 2012 and 2013. It also made three straight champions seeded seventh or lower.

For Harris, it was a rerun. In 2009 he was the No. 1 seed, the best team in the league, and he lost the final. In 2024 he was the No. 1 seed, the best team in the league, and he lost the final. Fifteen years, same ending.

The commissioner didn't escape. Hemby took Christian McCaffrey first overall and got 42 points out of him, the worst first-round return of the year. And Ethan Hess's Aaron Hernandez's Taxi Service went 3-10, scored a league-low 1,588 points and lost its last seven games.

## By the numbers

- **10.5**: the points Luke Martin lost the previous final by. He got them back with interest.
- **247**: Kyle Loy's first-round score, which sent the defending champion home.
- **$745**: Forrest's bid on Isaac Guerendo, the largest winning bid on record. He lost that week.
- **42**: points from Christian McCaffrey, the No. 1 overall pick. Commissioner's pick.
- **3**: teams that rostered Saquon Barkley.
- **7**: Luke Martin's seed, the third straight champion seeded seventh or lower.
