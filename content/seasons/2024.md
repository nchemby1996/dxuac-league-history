# Redemption, Chig Chig Style: Luke Martin Goes From Heartbreak to Hardware

Twelve months after losing the final, Luke Martin came back and won it, and nobody saw it coming in November. Chig Chig Time lost its opener to Kyle Loy 143-142, won four straight, then went 1-5 over the next six weeks, including a 90.5-point beating from Tanner Edwards. At 5-6 with two weeks left, he won both and climbed into the 7 seed at 7-6 with a 97.5 scoring index and no weekly high scores.

The regular season belonged to Tyler Harris. April Showers Bring Zay Flowers started 0-2, then went 9-2 the rest of the way. Harris led the league with 2,158 points (114.4 index), posted four weekly highs, and in week 5 beat Ethan Hess 233-92.5, the third-largest era-adjusted margin in league history. His draft was the best in the league (1.79 class index): Ja'Marr Chase at No. 5 was the most valuable pick of the year (+260 VOR), and Brian Thomas Jr. in round 10 added +120. James Brooks (8-5) and Drew Martin (8-5) took the next two byes. John Barrows took the last one at 7-5-1, thanks to a 144-144 tie with Loy in week 11.

The hard-luck story was Tanner Forrest. DEAN scored 2,052.5 points, second-most in the league, and still went 5-8. His -3.0 luck score is the fifth-worst in league history. In week 14 he made the biggest winning FAAB bid in the league's records: $745 of his $1,000 on Isaac Guerendo. Guerendo scored 26, and Forrest lost anyway.

The trade market ran on Saquon Barkley. Josh Book drafted him ninth overall, and he finished as the second-most valuable pick of the draft (+234). In week 9, Book sent him to Jesse Christman in an eight-player deal for Chuba Hubbard, Nick Chubb, DK Metcalf and D'Andre Swift. Saquon gave Christman 133 points in four weeks, 56 of them in week 12. In week 13 Christman flipped him to Brooks for Bijan Robinson and Courtland Sutton. In week 8, a week before the Saquon deal, Christman had sent CeeDee Lamb, his No. 2 overall pick, to Forrest along with Travis Kelce and Chris Olave. Christman's draft class ranked last (-1.64), and he still finished third. Book finished 12th, the worst season of his career.

Kyle Loy, the 11 seed, delivered the postseason's loudest number. He beat Edwards 247-156.5 in the first round, the highest winners-bracket score since at least 2012. Josh Allen scored 65, Jordan Addison 44 and Justin Jefferson 37. Drew Martin ended his run 206-185 in the quarterfinals. Luke Martin beat Forrest 176-132 and then handled Brooks 151.5-105. Christman beat Hess and then Barrows, 205.5-138.5. Harris got past Nate Hemby 161-143 and edged Christman 123-118.5 in the semifinals. Luke Martin's semifinal was a rout: 213.5-123.5 over Drew Martin, with Jonathan Taylor scoring 50 and Jayden Daniels 45.

In the final, the 7 seed beat the 1 seed 186.5-167. Daniels scored 38, Taylor 29 and the Eagles Coach 27.5, which was too much for Mike Evans (29), Brian Thomas Jr. (23) and Chase (21). It was Luke Martin's second title, after 2013. It was also the second time he lost a final and won the next one, after 2012-13. It made three straight champions seeded seventh or lower.

On draft day, Hemby took Christian McCaffrey first overall and got 42 points, -106 VOR, the worst first-round return of the year. Edwards's Isiah Pacheco (No. 10) and Christman's Olave (No. 23) both came in at -96. The value came later: Barrows found Lamar Jackson in round 5 (+217), Loy the Lions Coach in round 12 (+138) and Edwards Jared Goff in round 14 (+110).

The basement belonged to Hess. Aaron Hernandez's Taxi Service went 3-10, scored a league-low 1,588 points (84.2 index), posted four weekly lows and lost its last seven games. He also allowed the fourth-most points (by index) in league history.

- **3**: teams that rostered Saquon Barkley.
- **7**: Luke Martin's seed, making three straight champions seeded seventh or lower.
- **2,052.5**: Tanner Forrest's points, second in the league, in a 5-8 season.
