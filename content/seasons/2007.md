# The Heist at 7.5: Josh Book Steals the First One

Every dynasty needs an origin story. Josh Book's is a robbery.

First, understand the world it happened in. The first D.J.U.A.C. season was fantasy football as designed by a madman: a flex, a kicker, a head coach and a punter in every lineup, and scores that ran into the hundreds every week. The punters were the stars. Andy Lee scored 3,212 points, more than double Tom Brady's 1,462, and Brady was the best quarterback in the league. All eight teams spent their final-round pick on a punter. Ryan Hemby's, Dustin Colquitt, rated as the second-most valuable pick of the entire draft. Tyler Harris used the very last pick on Tom Malone, who scored zero. Lee himself went undrafted and turned up in Nate Hemby's lineup in Week 4.

Which is roughly when Nate's season turned up too. The Professor started 1-2, then won nine straight. Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay took first place for good in Week 8, finished 11-3, led the league in scoring and claimed the No. 1 seed. In Week 14 he hung 981 on Dylan Plouvier, which is still the highest raw single-week score in league history. In the semifinal he beat Ryan Hemby by 248.5. This was not a team. It was a coronation in progress.

Josh Book, meanwhile, was fine. His Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie went 9-5, took the No. 3 seed and posted a scoring index of 100.4, which is a statistician's way of saying "present." His draft class ranked seventh of eight. Nobody was building a shrine.

Then the bracket started, and fine turned out to be enough. Book beat Harris 735-639 in the first round and knocked off No. 2 Plouvier 670-612 in the semis. That set up the coronation, and Book crashed it. Joshy Woshy 797.5, Nate 790. In a season where teams averaged about 667 points a game, the first D.J.U.A.C. title came down to 7.5, the closest game of the whole postseason. It was the first of Book's seven championships. He has lost exactly one title game since. Nate would get his ring two years later. He'd also get beaten by Book in a final again.

If Nate had the cruelest finish, Kyle Loy had the cruelest season. Karl Loy started 0-7 and finished 3-11, dead last, despite ranking fourth in points. His luck rating of minus-3.72 wins is still the third-worst single season in league history, and it turned out to be a preview: no owner in league history has been unluckier over a career. He did get one moment. In Week 13 he dropped 947 on the eventual top seed, the second-highest score of the year, and handed Nate the loss that ended his streak.

Elsewhere, John Barrows found the best value in the draft, sixth-rounder Brian Westbrook at plus-719, and beat Curtiss Hemby 899-495 in Week 6, the biggest blowout of the year. Plouvier's second-round Marvin Harrison scored 216 points, the worst early pick in the draft. Plouvier still finished third, which is more than his receiver did for him.

## By the numbers

- **7.5**: Book's margin in the final, 797.5-790. Title No. 1 of 7.
- **981**: Nate's Week 14 score, still the highest raw single week in league history. It came with a lovely bye and no trophy.
- **0**: points from Tom Malone, Tyler Harris's punter and the last pick of the draft
- **3,212**: Andy Lee's points. Undrafted. Punter.
- **3-11**: Kyle Loy's record, with the fourth-most points in the league
- **100.4**: the champion's scoring index. Average has never looked so good.
