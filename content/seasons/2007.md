# Seven and a Half Points: Josh Book Steals the First D.J.U.A.C. Crown

Before anything else, understand the scale. In the league's first season, D.J.U.A.C. teams averaged about 667 points a game. Lineups had the usual skill players plus a flex, a D/ST, a kicker, a head coach and, yes, a punter. Punting was big business. Andy Lee led all punters with 3,212 points, more than double the 1,462 Tom Brady put up as the season's top quarterback, and at least nine punters cleared 2,000. The draft's final round was a punter round, with all eight teams taking one on picks 113 through 120. Ryan Hemby's 15th-rounder, Dustin Colquitt, scored 2,689 points and rated as the second-most valuable pick in the whole draft. Tyler Harris's punter, Tom Malone, scored zero. Lee went undrafted, and he turns up in Nate Hemby's lineup from Week 4 on.

The early weeks belonged to Curtiss Hemby, whose Pittsburg Gorillas opened 3-0, and then to Dylan Plouvier, whose Team panky stood 6-1 after Week 7. Nate Hemby started 1-2 and then won nine straight, from Week 4 through Week 12. He took first place for good in Week 8 and finished 11-3 as the No. 1 seed. Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay led the league with 10,759.5 points and posted six weekly high scores. In Week 14 he dropped 981 on Dylan Plouvier, which is still the highest raw single-week score in league history. Plouvier finished 10-4 with 10,596 points for the No. 2 seed.

Josh Book took the No. 3 seed at 9-5. His Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie had a scoring index of 100.4, about as average as a team can be. Six teams made the field and the top two seeds got byes. In the first round Book beat Tyler Harris's T Doggie 735-639, and Ryan Hemby's I Don't Knows edged Curtiss Hemby 668.5-628.5. In the semifinals Nate Hemby hung 942.5 on Ryan Hemby, winning by 248.5, while Book knocked off No. 2 Plouvier 670-612. The championship game was the closest of the whole postseason: Book 797.5, Nate Hemby 790. In a league where the average team scored about 667 a game, the first title came down to 7.5 points. Plouvier took third with a 798-674.5 win over Ryan Hemby.

The unluckiest team in the building was Kyle Loy's Karl Loy. Loy started 0-7 and finished 3-11, dead last, even though he ranked fourth in points with 9,018.5. His luck rating of minus-3.72 wins is the third-worst single season in league history. He still produced the season's second-highest score, 947 in Week 13, and the victim was the eventual top seed, Nate Hemby. Elsewhere, John Barrows' Johnny Boy beat Curtiss Hemby 899-495 in Week 6, a 404-point blowout and the biggest margin of the year. The season's low score was Curtiss Hemby's 251.5 in Week 5. The closest game was Ryan Hemby's 714.5-714 escape against Loy in Week 11.

The draft did not decide much. Book's class ranked seventh of eight by value over replacement, and he won the title anyway. Ryan Hemby's class ranked first, helped along by his punter. The best value of the draft was Barrows' sixth-round pick Brian Westbrook (1,209 points, +719 VOR). Barrows also took Adrian Peterson seventh overall in Peterson's rookie year, which returned 851 points. Nate Hemby's first-rounder, Shaun Alexander, went third overall and returned 325 points (-165 VOR), but his third-round pick Randy Moss scored 1,368, the most of any receiver. The worst early pick was Plouvier's second-rounder Marvin Harrison: 216 points, -552 VOR.
