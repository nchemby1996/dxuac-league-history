# Mediocre at Best, Champion at Last: John Barrows Wins a Title in His 13th Season

John Barrows named his 2020 team *Mediocre at Best*, and for twelve weeks that was exactly right. He went 6-6 with a 94.4 scoring index, a 45-63 all-play record and the ninth-ranked draft class of ten. His No. 2 overall pick, Christian McCaffrey, gave him 81 points and -67 of value. Then the 4-seed won three playoff games and the first championship of his 13 seasons. He's one of only five champions in league history to finish the regular season at .500 or worse.

The regular season belonged to Luke Martin. *Can you Diggs it* lost in Week 1 and then won 11 straight, a run that's still the third-longest winning streak in league history. Luke finished 11-1 with 1,957 points and a 117.6 scoring index, the third-highest season index on record. Tanner Forrest went 9-3 (1,853.5 points) for the No. 2 seed, and Drew Martin was 8-4. Then there was Kyle Loy. *No hope Pats* scored 1,785.5 points, the third-most in the league, and put up 255 against Barrows in Week 7. Measured against that week's average, it's the highest single-week score in league history. Kyle also faced a league-high 1,902.5 points against and finished 2-10. His -4.34 luck rating is the worst in the record book.

Josh Book had the reverse problem. He had the league's worst scoring index (88.3) and worst all-play record (32-76), and he still went 6-6 with a bye on +2.45 luck. Two of those wins came against Jesse Christman. In Week 6, ESPN shows no starters for Jesse, and Josh won 143-0, the most lopsided game in league history by era-adjusted margin. In Week 10 Josh won 51-29, the lowest-scoring win in the record book (era-adjusted). In Weeks 11 and 12, Josh and Jesse each took -60 commissioner penalties: Jesse went from 136 to 76 and from 117 to 57, and Josh from 143.5 to 83.5 and from 97.5 to 37.5. Three of those games were losses either way. Josh's Week 11 game wasn't. His raw 143.5 would have beaten Nate Hemby's 109.5, and the penalty turned it into a loss.

The title was won on the waiver wire. Tanner Forrest drafted Justin Jefferson in the 16th round and dropped him in Week 2. Barrows claimed him in Week 4 for $400, the second-biggest bid of the year, and got 159 points out of ten starts. Tanner did well on the wire himself. He paid $150 for Justin Herbert in Week 6, dropping Dak Prescott to make room, and Herbert gave him 200 points in nine starts. Jesse ran the trade market, with three deals in three weeks. The biggest was a six-player swap in Week 11: Dalvin Cook, Amari Cooper and Myles Gaskin to Tanner for Kenny Golladay, DeAndre Hopkins and Todd Gurley II. Gaskin had arrived from Drew a week earlier. Cook was Jesse's first-round pick and the third most valuable player in the draft (+184), and he scored 114 points in six starts for Tanner. Jesse's biggest bid, $500 for Le'Veon Bell, came in Week 16, when he was playing in the consolation bracket.

In Round 1, Nate beat Jesse 163-131.5, and Kyle, the 10-seed, beat Ethan Hess 156.5-109.5. Barrows routed Josh 177-130 in the quarterfinals (Jonathan Taylor, his fourth-round pick, scored 35). Tanner beat Kyle 157.5-130.5, Luke crushed Nate 160.5-70.5, and Drew got past Tyler Harris 150-141.5. In the semifinals, Barrows stopped Luke 166-152 behind 31 points from the Bills Coach, and Tanner beat Drew 184-172 with 32 from Herbert. In the final, Mike Evans scored 34 and Barrows beat Tanner 161-140.5. That's Tanner's third runner-up finish in five seasons, despite 32 from Gaskin. Luke took third, 163.5-132 over Drew.

Picks two through four all busted: Barrows's McCaffrey (-67), Drew's Saquon Barkley (No. 3, 11 points and -137) and Tyler's Michael Thomas (No. 4, -103). Tanner's Derrick Henry at No. 9 was the most valuable pick of the draft (+197), just ahead of Kyle's Alvin Kamara (+192). Kyle ended the year with 224 points in a Week 16 consolation game.

## By the numbers

- **6-6**: John Barrows's regular-season record, before three straight playoff wins
- **$400**: Barrows's Week 4 claim on Justin Jefferson, two weeks after Tanner Forrest dropped him
- **11**: straight wins for Luke Martin, who still lost in the semifinals
- **-4.34**: Kyle Loy's luck rating, the worst in league history, on the league's third-most points
- **143-0**: Josh Book over Jesse Christman in Week 6, when ESPN shows no starters for Jesse
- **-60**: each of four commissioner penalties in Weeks 11-12, one of which flipped Josh's win over Nate into a loss
