# Mediocre Until December

John Barrows named his 2020 team *Mediocre at Best*, and for twelve weeks it was the most honest team name in the league. He went 6-6. His draft class ranked near the bottom. His No. 2 overall pick was Christian McCaffrey, one year removed from being the most valuable pick in league history, and McCaffrey gave him 81 points. Then the 4-seed won three straight playoff games and the first championship of his 13 seasons.

The title was won on the waiver wire. Tanner Forrest drafted Justin Jefferson late and cut him almost immediately. Two weeks later Barrows claimed him for $400, the same price he'd paid a year earlier for one start of Bo Scarbrough. Same bid, slightly better return.

The regular season belonged to Luke Martin. Three years after a 2-10 season, *Can you Diggs it* lost its opener and then won 11 straight, finishing with the most points in the league. Tanner took the No. 2 seed behind Justin Herbert, a waiver pickup starring for a team called *Smokin' That Good Herb-ert*. At least he named the team after the right guy.

Kyle Loy got the opposite of 2019. A year after the luckiest season in league history, *No hope Pats* scored the third-most points, faced the most points against and went 2-10, the unluckiest season in league history. Kyle even hung 255 on Barrows, the highest single-week score ever measured against its week's average. The universe gives, and then it takes it all back with interest.

Josh Book had the reverse problem: the league's worst scoring index, and a .500 record with a bye anyway. He got a lot of help from Jesse Christman. In Week 6, ESPN shows no starters for Jesse, and Josh won 143-0, the most lopsided game in league history by era-adjusted margin. Then came the penalties: Josh and Jesse each took two from the commissioner late in the season. Three of those four games were losses either way. One of Josh's was not: his raw score would have beaten Nate Hemby, and the penalty turned it into a loss. Jesse, still calling his team *2X Champ*, finished 4-8. His biggest bid of the year was $500 on Le'Veon Bell, spent in the consolation bracket. Two seasons after Bell went No. 1 overall and never started for Luke, he was still finding ways to cost people.

The playoffs were where the name stopped fitting. Barrows routed Josh in the quarterfinals, then met the 11-1 juggernaut and beat Luke with 31 points from the Bills Coach, of all things. In the final, Mike Evans scored 34 and Barrows beat Tanner 161-140.5.

That's Tanner's third runner-up finish in five seasons. In 2016 he lost to a losing-record Jesse. In 2018 he lost to a team that started 0-4. In 2020 he lost to a team that named itself *Mediocre at Best* and was right for twelve weeks. Barrows became one of only five champions in league history to finish the regular season at .500 or worse, and the only one who had warned everybody in advance.

The top of the draft was a crater. Picks two through four all busted, with Drew Martin's Saquon Barkley managing 11 points all year. Tanner's Derrick Henry was the best pick of the draft. It didn't buy him a ring either.

## By the numbers

- **13**: seasons John Barrows played before his first title
- **$400**: Barrows's bid on Justin Jefferson, the same amount that bought him 6 points of Bo Scarbrough a year earlier
- **11**: straight wins for Luke Martin, who still lost to the 6-6 team in the semifinals
- **-4.34**: Kyle Loy's luck, the worst in league history, one season after the best
- **143-0**: Josh Book over Jesse Christman in Week 6, when ESPN shows no starters for Jesse
- **143.5**: Josh's raw score in Week 11, enough to beat Nate Hemby's 109.5 until a -60 penalty made it a loss
