# T Doggie Survives the Free-for-All: Tyler Harris Goes 9-1-1 and Finishes the Job

Year two looked like a different league. D.J.U.A.C. grew to 11 teams, the punter and the flex were gone, and scores came back to earth: the average team scored 158.7 points a week, down from about 667. With an odd number of teams, someone sat out every week, so most teams played 11 regular-season games and Tanner Forrest played 10. When the regular season ended, every team made the playoff bracket and the top five seeds got byes.

Tyler Nystrom's Team Saltines opened 5-0. Nate Hemby's Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay opened 6-0 before losing three of its last five to finish 8-3. The team nobody could put away was Tyler Harris's T Doggie. Harris lost only once all season, 238.5-127 to Josh Book in Week 2. His only other blemish was a 162.5-162.5 tie with Curtiss Hemby in Week 8. He finished 9-1-1, edged 9-2 Nystrom for the No. 1 seed, and posted the fourth-best regular-season record in league history (.864). He also saved his best for last: 275.5 against Nate Hemby in Week 12, the highest score of the season. Josh Book led the league with 2,043 points, a scoring index of 117.2 that still ranks fourth all time, but his 7-4 record left him the No. 4 seed.

Seeds six through 11 played in the opening round. Zak Hensley beat Tanner Forrest 183.5-109, Kyle Loy beat Dylan Plouvier 178-160, and No. 9 Curtiss Hemby upset No. 8 Brandon Mlekus 173.5-159.5. The quarterfinals produced the bracket's one real shock. Hensley, a 5-6 sixth seed, beat No. 3 Nate Hemby 173-153.5. The favorites held everywhere else: Book crushed John Barrows 245-124, Harris beat Curtiss Hemby 188.5-153, and Nystrom beat Loy 188-157.5. Hensley's luck ran out in the semifinals, where Nystrom beat him 196.5-108. Harris ended Book's season 208-155. In the final Harris beat Nystrom 196.5-174, and the No. 1 seed won the title. Hensley took third with a 180.5-168.5 win over Book.

The draft explains plenty. Harris got the most valuable pick of the year, Drew Brees at No. 18 in the second round: 404 points, +224 VOR. Nystrom drafted the best class in the league, with eighth-rounder Michael Turner (394 points) and ninth-rounder Andre Johnson (413) doing the heavy lifting, and his class index of 2.02 led the season. The busts were brutal. Nate Hemby's second-round pick Laurence Maroney scored 12 points (-223 VOR), which still stands as the biggest early-round bust in league history. Kyle Loy took Tom Brady third overall and got 2 points; Brady's season famously ended in Week 1. Barrows' third-rounder Carson Palmer managed 11.

Down at the bottom, Tanner Forrest's Team Awesomeness started 0-7 and finished 1-9. Its scoring index of 77.3 is the second-lowest in league history, and its draft class (-1.99) ranked last of 11. Team Awesomeness did not go quietly in the consolation games, though. After the first-round loss it scored 188 points in each of its last two games, beating Mlekus 188-121.5 and Curtiss Hemby 188-163 to finish seventh. Mlekus, the No. 8 seed, finished 11th. The worst score of the year was Loy's 62 against Harris in Week 10, which also produced the year's biggest margin, 135.5 points. Nate Hemby got the narrowest win of the year, 164.5-164 over Hensley in Week 6, which is the second-narrowest victory in league history.

## By the numbers

- **9-1-1**: Tyler Harris's regular-season record, the fourth-best in league history (.864)
- **275.5**: Harris's Week 12 score against Nate Hemby, the season high
- **2,043**: points scored by Josh Book, the league high, which got him only the No. 4 seed
- **12**: points from Laurence Maroney, Nate Hemby's second-round pick and the biggest early-round bust in league history
- **0.5**: Nate Hemby's margin over Zak Hensley in Week 6, 164.5-164
- **188 and 188**: Tanner Forrest's scores in his two consolation wins, after a 1-9 season
