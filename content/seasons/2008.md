# The Only One: T Doggie Goes 9-1-1 and Cashes It In

Tyler Harris has won exactly one D.J.U.A.C. championship. This is the story of it. Savor it, because the sequel has been in development for 17 seasons.

Year two was a different league. It grew to 11 teams, the punter and the flex were gone, and scores came back to Earth. With an odd number of teams somebody sat out every week, and when the regular season ended, every single team made the bracket. Participation trophies, but with a bracket.

T Doggie was the team nobody could put away. Harris lost once all year, 238.5-127 to Josh Book in Week 2, and tied Curtiss Hemby 162.5-162.5 in Week 8. Everything else was a win. He finished 9-1-1, took the No. 1 seed and posted the fourth-best regular-season record in league history. In Week 12 he dropped 275.5 on Nate Hemby, the season high, apparently just to make a point.

Nate needed the reminder. The Professor opened 6-0, then lost three of his last five, then lost in the quarterfinals to Zak Hensley, a 5-6 sixth seed. Back in Week 6, Nate had beaten Hensley by half a point. Hensley got the last laugh 173-153.5, finished third, and never played another D.J.U.A.C. season. Nate's draft was worse than his playoffs. His second-round pick, Laurence Maroney, scored 12 points, still the biggest early-round bust in league history. Kyle Loy took Tom Brady third overall and got 2 points; Brady's season ended in Week 1. Two of the five worst early picks the league has ever seen, one draft.

Book had the opposite problem. He led the league with 2,043 points, a scoring index that still ranks fourth all time, and his 7-4 record got him the No. 4 seed for his trouble. He crushed John Barrows 245-124 in the quarterfinals and then drew Harris in the semifinal. Book was the only team to beat T Doggie all year. Harris returned the favor, 208-155.

That left Tyler Nystrom, a one-season wonder of the highest order. His Team Saltines opened 5-0, went 9-2 and drafted the best class in the league, with eighth-rounder Michael Turner and ninth-rounder Andre Johnson doing the heavy lifting. He beat Hensley 196.5-108 to reach the final. Harris beat him 196.5-174. Nystrom never played another season. One year, one final, one silver medal, gone.

So the No. 1 seed won the title, something it has done only four times in league history. Harris had the most valuable pick of the draft, Drew Brees at No. 18, and the regular season to match. For once, the chalk held.

At the other end, meet Tanner Forrest. In his first D.J.U.A.C. season, Team Awesomeness started 0-7 and finished 1-9, with the second-lowest scoring index in league history and the worst draft class of 11. Then, with nothing left to play for, it scored 188 points in each of its last two consolation games and climbed to seventh. Forrest would later reach five title games and lose all five. The big-game losing came later. In 2008 he just lost the small ones.

The low point of the year belonged to Loy, who scored 62 against Harris in Week 10 and lost by 135.5, the biggest margin of the season. Brandon Mlekus entered the bracket as the No. 8 seed and finished 11th, which takes real effort.

## By the numbers

- **9-1-1**: Harris's record, .864, fourth-best in league history
- **1**: championships Harris has won. You just read about it.
- **12**: points from Laurence Maroney, Nate's second-rounder and the biggest early-round bust in league history
- **2,043**: Book's league-leading points, good for the No. 4 seed
- **0.5**: Nate's Week 6 margin over Zak Hensley, 164.5-164. Hensley collected in the quarterfinals.
- **188 and 188**: Tanner Forrest's consolation scores, after a 1-9 season
