# The Flounders Don't Flounder: Drew Martin Loses His No. 2 Pick and Wins It All Anyway

The 2017 season gave Drew Martin every excuse. His first-round pick, David Johnson, went No. 2 overall and returned 8 points for the year, -136 points of value over replacement, which ranked 182nd of the 190 players drafted. The Fumbling Flounders started 6-0 anyway, finished 10-2, took the No. 1 seed and led the league with 1,714 points (scoring index 112.6). The man who kept them afloat came out of the third round. Todd Gurley, the 22nd pick, scored 348 points, was worth +204 over replacement and was the single most valuable player in the 2017 draft. The record book still lists him as the fourth-most valuable pick in league history.

The race behind Drew was tight. Josh Book went 9-3 with the second-most points (1,670) and drew the No. 3 seed, because division winner Jesse Christman (8-4, *My Name is Jeff...ery*) took No. 2. Josh's draft carried him. Antonio Brown at No. 3 overall was worth +115, and a 10th-round flier on Kareem Hunt (pick 98) returned 283 points and +139 of value. Hunt scored 43 in Week 1 as Josh opened with a 179-84.5 beating of Nate Hemby, and that pick is the fourth-best late-round selection in the record book. Nate (8-4, No. 4) and John Barrows (7-5, No. 5) made up the rest of the bye club. Tanner Forrest got the sixth and final bye despite going 5-7. The regular season's biggest blowout belonged to the champion-in-waiting: Drew 184, Ethan Hess 82.5 in Week 6, a 101.5-point margin.

The bracket opened in Week 13 with the bottom four seeds. Tyler Harris edged Kyle Loy 122-118, and Ethan Hess, playing his first season in the league, blasted Luke Martin 139-49.5. The quarterfinals produced the season's scare. Drew survived Tyler Harris 139-137, a two-point escape for the top seed. John Barrows knocked out Nate 152.5-117.5, Josh handled Tanner 144.5-96.5, and Jesse got past Ethan 131.5-121.

Then Gurley took over. He scored 49 in the semifinal as Drew put up 192.5 and buried Barrows by 81.5 points. He scored 48 more in the championship game, and Drew beat Josh Book 160.5-137.5 for his first title in his sixth season. That's 97 points from one running back over the two biggest weeks of the year. It also handed Josh the first title-game loss of his career after he'd gone 5-0 in finals (2007, 2011, 2012, 2014, 2015). Jesse beat Barrows 127.5-112 for third. Drew joined 2008, 2014 and 2015 as the only years in which the No. 1 seed won the whole thing.

Drew wasn't the only one with a ruined first round. Luke Martin's Odell Beckham Jr. (No. 4 overall) produced 58 points and -84 of value. Ethan's second-round Aaron Rodgers (No. 12) finished at -136, tied with Johnson for the worst value of any pick in the first two rounds. Barrows's second-rounder Terrelle Pryor Sr. came in at -105.

At the bottom of the table, Luke Martin's *CANYOUDIGGIT TERPS* went 2-10. They opened 2-1 with the season-high 186 against Nate in Week 3, then lost their last nine games. The streak reached ten in the next season's opener and is the second-longest losing streak in league history. Luke's luck rating was -3.0 (the fourth-unluckiest season on record), and his draft class ranked last of ten and fourth-worst all-time. Kyle Loy and Tyler Harris both went 3-9. Kyle also absorbed a -15 commissioner adjustment in Week 11 that turned 113 into 98, but he'd have lost to Tanner Forrest's 115.5 either way. Ethan's debut team, *My Nut Zach Ertz*, posted the league's lowest scoring index (87.5) and still went 5-7 and won a playoff game.

## By the numbers

- **10-2**: Drew Martin's record, with a league-best 1,714 points and the No. 1 seed
- **97**: Todd Gurley's points in the semifinal (49) and championship (48)
- **8**: points from David Johnson, the No. 2 overall pick (-136 value over replacement)
- **5-0**: Josh Book's record in title games before this loss
- **9**: straight losses to end Luke Martin's regular season
- **2**: Drew's quarterfinal margin over Tyler Harris (139-137), the closest call of his title run
