# Eight Points and a Ring

The 2017 season handed Drew Martin the best excuse in fantasy football. He took David Johnson No. 2 overall, and David Johnson gave him 8 points. Not 8 a week. Eight for the year. Most owners would have spent the fall complaining in the group chat. Drew started 6-0.

The man who bailed him out came out of the third round. Todd Gurley was the most valuable player in the draft and still ranks among the best picks in league history. With Gurley doing the heavy lifting, *The Fumbling Flounders* led the league in scoring and took the No. 1 seed. The excuse was available. Drew declined it.

Josh Book, meanwhile, spent the year looking like Josh Book. He found Kareem Hunt in the 10th round, beat the commissioner by nearly a hundred in the opener and arrived in December with a perfect record in title games. Five played, five won. Nobody was betting against him. Behind them, Tanner Forrest earned a first-round bye at 5-7, which tells you everything about a bracket that let in all ten teams.

The playoffs nearly ended Drew's story in the first act. Tyler Harris, still hunting a second title nine years after his first, took the top seed to the wire in the quarterfinals and lost 139-137. Nobody got closer. Then Gurley took over. He scored 49 in the semifinal and 48 in the final, and Drew beat Josh 160.5-137.5 for his first championship. Josh had never lost a title game until a third-round running back took one from him. It was also only the fourth time a No. 1 seed had won the whole thing, which says plenty about how this league treats favorites. The Flounders lost their first-round pick and didn't flounder. Verdict: the best excuse in the league, wasted on a champion.

Jesse Christman's title defense ended in the semifinals, against Josh, and he settled for third. Nate Hemby lost his quarterfinal to John Barrows, ending the commissioner's season the way it began: getting handled.

David Johnson had company in the first-round graveyard. Luke Martin's Odell Beckham Jr. went No. 4 and returned next to nothing. Ethan Hess's second-round Aaron Rodgers tied Johnson for the worst value of any early pick. Barrows spent his second-rounder on Terrelle Pryor Sr. That's the whole sentence.

Luke wore it worst. *CANYOUDIGGIT TERPS* opened 2-1, posted the season high against Nate, then never won again. His draft class ranked last, his luck ranked among the worst ever, and his playoff game was a 139-49.5 loss to Ethan, a first-year owner with the league's lowest scoring index. Kyle Loy even took a commissioner penalty one week. He'd have lost anyway.

## By the numbers

- **8**: points from David Johnson, the No. 2 overall pick. The Flounders won the title anyway.
- **97**: Todd Gurley's points in the semifinal and final combined
- **5-0**: Josh Book's record in title games before he met Drew
- **2**: Tyler Harris's margin of defeat against the eventual champion. So close, so Tyler.
- **10**: Luke Martin's losing streak, nine to finish this season and one more to open next season, the second-longest in league history
- **190**: players drafted. Only seven returned less value than David Johnson.
