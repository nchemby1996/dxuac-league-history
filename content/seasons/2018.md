# The Professor Rises From 0-4

On the morning of Week 5, Nate Hemby was 0-4 and owned the lowest regular-season score of the year. The commissioner's team looked like a cautionary tale. By Week 16 it was holding the trophy. Nobody in 2018 took a stranger road to a ring.

The turnaround started with a trade. Nate sent Rob Gronkowski and Kerryon Johnson to Drew Martin for Calvin Ridley and Marshawn Lynch, and *Moosey Moosey Moosey* promptly won six straight. It crawled to 7-5, grabbed the No. 4 seed and a bye, and still tied for the most weekly low scores in the league. Nobody was scared. That's not the résumé of a champion. It was about to be.

The rest of the league spent the fall proving that points and wins are different things. Josh Book led the league in scoring and took the No. 1 seed, as Josh does. Tanner Forrest landed the No. 2 seed with a below-average scoring index and the most waiver adds in the league. Ethan Hess scored the third-most points, posted the most weekly high scores, and missed the bye anyway. Ethan did the work. The schedule didn't care.

The draft is remembered for its first pick. Luke Martin took Le'Veon Bell No. 1 overall. Bell sat on Luke's bench for ten weeks without starting a single game, then got dropped. Luke's season opened with a loss that stretched his losing streak from the year before to ten, and it got weirder from there, including a dead-even tie with Jesse Christman. Josh, meanwhile, found Patrick Mahomes in the fourth round. (ESPN has since re-scored 2018 player points, so this book doesn't cite individual totals or draft values for the season.)

The playoffs were where Nate settled old business. In the quarterfinals he got John Barrows, the man who had bounced him a year earlier, and survived. In the semifinals he held the top-seeded Josh to 88 and won. Drew, who had handed Nate the trade that started all this, lost his semifinal to Tanner and finished fourth. No good deed.

The final wasn't close. Nate saved his best score of the playoffs for last and beat Tanner 177-139 for his second championship, and his first since 2009. Two years after losing the 2016 final to Jesse, Tanner had lost another one. Hold that thought.

At the bottom, Kyle Loy's *Jimmy G-string* won its opener, lost seven straight and finished as the bottom seed with the league's lowest scoring index. Jesse's *Male pattern Baldwiness* tied the champion for the most weekly low scores. Only one of them got a ring for it.

Verdict: Nate went 0-4, made one trade and won it all. The Professor still had it.

## By the numbers

- **0-4**: Nate Hemby's start. Then six straight wins and a title.
- **9**: years between Nate's championships (2009 and 2018)
- **91**: Nate's Week 2 score against Tanner Forrest, the lowest of the regular season, from the eventual champion
- **0**: games Le'Veon Bell started for Luke Martin after going No. 1 overall
- **0.5**: Drew Martin's winning margin over Ethan Hess in Week 3, the closest game of the year
- **236**: Tanner Forrest's Week 4 score, the season high. He still lost the final.
