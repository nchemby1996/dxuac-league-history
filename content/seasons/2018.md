# From 0-4 to the Crown: Moosey Moosey Moosey Ends a Nine-Year Wait

On the morning of Week 5, Nate Hemby was 0-4 and had just posted the season's lowest score, a 91 against Tanner Forrest in Week 2. By Week 16 he was holding his second championship and his first since 2009. No team in 2018 took a stranger route to the title.

The scoring went up across the board. The average team score per game jumped from about 127 in 2017 to nearly 142, and the top of the table piled up points. Josh Book went 9-3, led the league with 1,870 points (scoring index 110.0) and took the No. 1 seed. Drew Martin scored 1,865 but went just 7-5 and slipped to No. 3. He also won the narrowest game of the year, 148.5-148 over Ethan Hess in Week 3, the third-narrowest victory in league history. Ethan was the season's hard-luck case. *Tittsburgh Stealers* scored 1,862.5 (third-most), posted a league-high four weekly high scores and still finished 5-7. His -1.72 luck was the worst in the league, and he missed the bye. Tanner Forrest went the other way. He finished 7-5 with a below-average scoring index (99.4) and a 47-61 all-play record, yet took the No. 2 seed. Along the way he dropped the season's biggest number, 236 against Kyle Loy in Week 4.

Nate's turnaround started the same week as the first of the season's two four-player trades. Before Week 5 he sent Rob Gronkowski and Kerryon Johnson to Drew Martin for Calvin Ridley and Marshawn Lynch. Moosey Moosey Moosey then won six straight from Weeks 5 through 10, finished 7-5 and grabbed the No. 4 seed and a first-round bye. Drew was the busiest dealer of the year, part of three of the season's five trades (Chris Hogan for Peyton Barber with Josh in Week 2, and Nyheim Hines for the Packers Coach with Ethan in Week 9). Tanner and Luke Martin made the other big swap in Week 8: Deshaun Watson and Taylor Gabriel went to Luke for Amari Cooper and Andy Dalton. This was the last season before FAAB. Tanner worked the waiver wire hardest, with 57 executed adds against Drew's 40.

The draft is best remembered for its first pick. Luke Martin took Le'Veon Bell No. 1 overall, and Bell sat out the entire NFL season. Luke's *Need a little Juju* still scraped to 5-6-1, with a Week 2 tie against Jesse Christman at 152.5 apiece. Josh found Patrick Mahomes in the fourth round (pick 31), the year Mahomes threw 50 touchdowns and won the MVP. (ESPN has since re-scored 2018 player points, so this book doesn't cite individual player totals or draft values for the season.)

In the playoffs, Jesse edged Tyler Harris 148.5-144 in Round 1, and Ethan beat Kyle 130-99.5. Ethan's reward was Tanner, who beat him 181-142.5. Nate survived John Barrows 113.5-108 in the quarterfinals, Josh beat Jesse 134-98.5, and Drew got past Luke 156-146. The semifinals belonged to the lower seeds. Nate held the top-seeded Josh to 88 and won 109-88, and Tanner beat Drew 134-131.5. In the championship, Nate saved his best for last and beat Tanner 177-139. Tanner lost his second title game in three seasons, after 2016. Josh beat Drew 144.5-141 for third.

At the bottom, Kyle Loy's *Jimmy G-string* won its opener and then lost seven straight. Kyle finished 4-8, last in the standings, on the league's lowest scoring index (91.4). Jesse's *Male pattern Baldwiness* went 4-7-1 and tied for the most weekly low scores (three) with, of all teams, the eventual champion.

## By the numbers

- **0-4**: Nate Hemby's start, followed by a six-game winning streak and a title
- **9**: years between Nate's championships (2009 and 2018)
- **88**: the No. 1 seed Josh Book's score in his semifinal loss to Nate
- **236**: Tanner Forrest's Week 4 score, the season high
- **0.5**: Drew Martin's winning margin over Ethan Hess in Week 3 (148.5-148)
- **1,862.5**: points scored by Ethan Hess, who went 5-7 and missed the bye
