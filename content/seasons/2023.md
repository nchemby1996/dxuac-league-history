# By One Point: Tanner Edwards Takes the Long Way to His First Title

Josh Book spent the 2023 regular season making it look easy. Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie won its first four games, lost only to John Barrows in week 5 and Drew Martin in week 10, and finished 11-2, the fifth-best record in league history and the No. 1 seed. It wasn't dominance so much as timing. Book's 102.8 scoring index was only sixth-best in the league, he never once posted the week's high score, and his 3.28 wins above all-play expectation rank as the third-luckiest season on record. The real scoring machine was James Brooks, whose Twiggity Twiggity led the league with 2,107 points (110.9 index) and took the 3 seed. Barrows was 9-4 and seeded second, and Tyler Harris rounded out the byes at 8-5.

Tanner Edwards didn't look like a contender. Kitties and Samsquanches lost four of five between weeks 8 and 12 and sat at 5-7 going into the final week. Then Edwards put up 248.5 on Kyle Loy, the season's highest score, in a 135-point blowout that ranks as the fifth-largest margin in league history once you adjust for era. He finished 6-7 and took the 8 seed. His record undersold him: 1,993.5 points, a 104.9 index and 1.05 fewer wins than his all-play mark called for.

It was a quiet year on the wire and in the trade market. The league made only two trades, both involving Tanner Forrest. The biggest FAAB bid was Jesse Christman's $508 for Trey McBride in week 15. Tanner Forrest spent $501 on Jerome Ford in week 3, and Barrows landed rookie Puka Nacua for $200 in week 2.

Edwards opened the playoffs by beating Drew Martin 191.5-97. Then came the game of the season: the 8 seed against the 11-2 top seed. Edwards won 151.5-150.5. His 19th-round pick, Sam LaPorta, scored 28. Book's Gabe Davis and Dalton Kincaid scored zero, and Edwards survived even with -1.5 points from his Jaguars Coach. It was the second straight season the No. 1 seed lost its first playoff game. The rest of the quarterfinals were lopsided. Nate Hemby, the 7 seed, ran over Barrows 190.5-105.5, and Harris beat Loy 179-125. Loy had stunned Christman 136.5-128 as the 12 seed in round one. The exception was Luke Martin, who beat Brooks 136.5-134 and ended the league's highest-scoring season. In the semifinals Amari Cooper scored 58 as Edwards beat Harris 149-128, and Luke Martin beat Hemby 177.5-142.5.

The final matched two 6-7 teams, Edwards against Luke Martin's Praise the sun god Amon-Ra. Lamar Jackson scored 50 and De'Von Achane 25, and Edwards won 180-169.5, even though Luke Martin got 32 from DJ Moore, a player he drafted in six different seasons from 2018 to 2024. Edwards, in his third season, became the ninth different owner to win the league. He was also the second straight 8 seed to win it, and he did it with three playoff wins by a combined 32.5 points after the opening rout.

Luke Martin had the best draft, ranked first at 1.62, with Amon-Ra St. Brown in round 2 (+183 VOR) and Nico Collins in round 14 (+107). The best late value belonged to the champion: LaPorta, the 220th pick, returned +109. The worst early pick was Book's second-rounder Nick Chubb, who was hurt in week 2, scored 24 points and finished at -143 VOR. Justin Jefferson, taken first overall by Christman, returned just +47.

The bottom of the table was all Loy. Ambulance went 2-11 with a seven-game losing streak from week 2 through week 8, 1,592.5 points (83.8 index), four weekly low scores and the fifth-most points allowed (by index) in league history. Forrest's Downs Bad went 4-9 and finished 12th.
