# Named It, Claimed It

Ten years earlier, Jesse Christman went 1-12 in his first season and became the league's punchline. In 2016 he won a title from a losing record. In 2019 he showed up with one ring and a team called *2X Champ*, which is either confidence or a dare. By December it was just accurate.

Jesse was only the No. 3 seed, and nobody got within nine points of him in the playoffs. Tanner Forrest went first, then Drew Martin, then Luke Martin in the final. His first-round pick, Michael Thomas, was steady all month. His best move cost nothing. Jesse picked up Ryan Fitzpatrick off the street in Week 16, started him in the championship game and got 40 points. Jesse won 161-124, and Luke fell to 1-3 in title games. Welcome back to the podium, Jesse. Bring the name tag.

This was the first FAAB season, and the league spent its fake money like it was real. The biggest bid of the year was John Barrows's $400 on Bo Scarbrough. It bought one start and 6 points. Drew emptied nearly his whole budget. Tyler Harris barely touched his, which may be the most fiscally responsible thing anyone did in a season that gave him nothing back.

Then there was Kyle Loy. *country roads take mahomes* scored the fewest points in the league and had the worst all-play record. They also started 5-0 and took a bye. That's the luckiest season in league history, and the bracket kept the gifts coming. Kyle knocked out Josh Book by three points, lost to Luke and then beat Drew for third place. The lowest-scoring team in the league finished third. Drew scored the most points in the league and finished fourth. Fantasy football is a fair and just game.

Josh had it worse. He drafted Christian McCaffrey at No. 3 overall and got the most valuable pick in league history. He still lost to Kyle.

Nate Hemby had the steal of the year, Lamar Jackson in the 12th round, the second-best late-round pick the league has ever seen. It got the commissioner a 6-6 record and the No. 7 seed. His first-round pick was David Johnson, the same David Johnson who gave Drew 8 points two years earlier, and it lost value again. Nate did beat Tyler twice, scoring exactly 160 both times, before Drew sent him home.

The busts had a theme. Jesse's third-round Antonio Brown, the same Antonio Brown Josh took No. 3 overall in 2017, scored 12 points. Kyle took Saquon Barkley No. 1 overall and got a perfectly fine season, which isn't what anyone wants from the top pick.

At the bottom, Tyler's *The Horny Buffalos* opened 0-6, finished 2-10 with the worst luck in the league, and lost their playoff game by 80. Ethan Hess went 3-9 but won a playoff game before Luke ran him over. And Kyle, naturally, posted the lowest score of the season. Then finished third.

## By the numbers

- **1-12**: Jesse Christman's record in 2009. Two titles later, he's the one naming teams.
- **40**: points from Ryan Fitzpatrick, a Week 16 free agent, in the title game
- **+3.62**: Kyle Loy's luck rating, the best in league history, for the team with the fewest points
- **$400**: the biggest bid of the first FAAB season. It bought Bo Scarbrough and 6 points.
- **115**: the pick Nate Hemby spent on Lamar Jackson, who scored 486 points
- **$52**: Tyler Harris's FAAB spending for the entire season. Drew spent $995.
