# Same Script, Bigger Margin: Josh Book Ends Nate Hemby's Perfect Run in the Final

The league went back to eight teams in 2011, and John Barrows returned after a year away. The playoff field shrank to four with no byes. With 12 regular-season weeks and the season running through Week 16, each playoff round covered two NFL weeks, which is why the postseason scores look about twice as big. During the regular season, teams averaged about 123 points a week.

For ten weeks Nate Hemby was perfect. His Taste Dwayne Bowe won its first 10 games, and together with the five straight wins that closed his 2010 season, that made 15 consecutive regular-season wins, still the longest streak in league history. One of those wins was as close as it gets: 115.5-115 over Josh Book in Week 4. The streak ended in Week 11 when Book beat him 139.5-121.5, and Brandon Mlekus beat him 130-122.5 the next week. Even so, Hemby finished 10-2 as the No. 1 seed with 1,661 points and five weekly high scores.

Book's season was bumpier. He opened 3-0, sank to 4-5 after Week 9, and then won his last three. His Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie finished 7-5. That was one of three 7-5 records, alongside Mlekus' THE BEAST MODE IS ON and Seth Chandler's Team sethinator, and Book was the No. 3 seed. But no one scored more. Book led the league with 1,677 points and a 113.7 scoring index, and he had the season's highest regular-season score, 207 against Jesse Christman in Week 7. Adjusted for era, that's the second-highest single-week score in league history. He also had the third-highest, 188.5 against Tyler Harris in Week 12.

In the two-week semifinals Hemby flattened No. 4 Chandler 388-215.5, and Book beat No. 2 Mlekus 312.5-259.5. That set up a rematch of the very first D.J.U.A.C. title game, with Book again the No. 3 seed and Hemby again the No. 1. This one wasn't close. Book scored 328.5 to Hemby's 175.5 and won by 153. That was his second championship, both of them over the same top seed. Mlekus beat Chandler 254-226 for third.

Mlekus didn't get a title, but he got history: his draft class index of 2.48 is the best in league history, led by Tom Brady at No. 3 overall (378 points, +135 VOR). Book's title team was built on second-round Drew Brees (429 points, +186) and seventh-round Wes Welker (+117). Hemby's first-rounder LeSean McCoy returned +148, and his 14th-round Packers Coach returned 339 points (+145.5), one of the five best late-round picks in league history. His second-rounder, Andre Johnson, managed 63 points. The worst pick belonged to Christman, who took Jamaal Charles fifth overall and got 15 points (-149 VOR), the fourth-biggest early-round bust in league history. Three picks later, Dennis Hensley took Aaron Rodgers at No. 8, and Rodgers' 432 points made him the most valuable pick of the draft.

Christman had a strange year. He started 0-5 and finished 4-8 despite ranking third in points (1,478.5). John Barrows finished last at 3-9, and his luck rating of minus-1.93 wins was the worst in the league. The season's most lopsided game came in Week 8, when Chandler beat Hensley 159.5-47. Hensley's 47 was the low score of the year, and the 112.5-point margin, adjusted for era, is among the five biggest in league history. In the consolation bracket, Harris beat Christman 266.5-266.

## By the numbers

- **15**: Nate Hemby's regular-season winning streak, 2010 Week 9 through 2011 Week 10, the league record
- **328.5-175.5**: the championship game, Josh Book's second title and his second over a No. 1-seeded Nate Hemby
- **207**: Book's Week 7 score, the second-highest single-week score in league history once adjusted for era
- **2.48**: Brandon Mlekus' draft class index, the best in league history
- **159.5-47**: Seth Chandler's Week 8 rout of Dennis Hensley
- **0.5**: Hemby's margin over Book in their Week 4 meeting, 115.5-115
