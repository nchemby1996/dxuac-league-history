# Fifteen Straight, Then Josh Book: The First Final, Only Worse

For ten weeks in 2011, Nate Hemby could not lose. Then he ran into the one owner he has never beaten in a final.

The league went back to eight teams, John Barrows returned after a year away, and the playoffs shrank to four teams with two-week rounds, which is why the postseason scores look about twice as big. None of that slowed down Taste Dwayne Bowe. Nate won his first 10 games. Add the five straight that closed his 2010 season and that's 15 consecutive regular-season wins, still the longest streak in league history. Not all of them were pretty. In Week 4 he beat Josh Book 115.5-115. Book would get the last word, twice.

In Week 11, Book ended the streak 139.5-121.5. A week later Brandon Mlekus beat Nate too. It didn't matter much: Nate finished 10-2 with five weekly high scores and the No. 1 seed.

Book's season, meanwhile, was a mess until it wasn't. Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie opened 3-0, sank to 4-5 after Week 9 and then won its last three to finish 7-5, one of three 7-5 teams, and grab the No. 3 seed. Nobody scored more. Book led the league in points and had the best regular-season week of the year, 207 against Jesse Christman in Week 7, which adjusted for era is the second-highest single-week score in league history. The record said 7-5. The scoreboard said otherwise.

In the semifinals Nate flattened Seth Chandler 388-215.5 and Book beat Mlekus 312.5-259.5. That set up the rematch of the very first D.J.U.A.C. title game: Book as the No. 3 seed, Nate as the No. 1, exactly like 2007. In 2007 Book won by seven and a half points. This time he won by 153. Book 328.5, Nate 175.5. That's two finals between them, two Book wins, and a margin that went from heartbreaker to demolition. It was Book's second title, and the first half of the first of his three back-to-backs.

Mlekus didn't get a ring, but he got history. His draft class is the best in league history, led by Tom Brady at No. 3 overall. He finished third. Book's title team was built on second-round Drew Brees and seventh-round Wes Welker. Nate's first-rounder, LeSean McCoy, delivered; his second-rounder, Andre Johnson, scored 63 points. His best late pick was a 14th-round Packers Coach worth 339 points, still one of the five best late-round picks in league history, which tells you something about head coaches in 2011 and nothing good about Andre Johnson.

Then there was Christman, who took Jamaal Charles fifth overall and got 15 points, the fourth-biggest early-round bust in league history. Three picks later, Dennis Hensley took Aaron Rodgers, who became the most valuable pick of the draft. Christman started 0-5, finished 4-8 despite ranking third in points, and won his last two games. Those two wins turned out to be the start of a 13-game winning streak that ran into next season, the second-longest in league history. Second to Nate's, naturally.

At the bottom, Barrows' return went about how returns usually go: last place at 3-9, with the worst luck rating in the league. Hensley scored 47 in a Week 8 loss to Chandler, the low of the year and one of the five most lopsided games in league history once adjusted for era. And in the consolation bracket, Harris beat Christman 266.5-266, the kind of finish that deserved a bigger stage.

## By the numbers

- **15**: Nate's regular-season winning streak, the league record
- **153**: the margin of the final. In 2007 it was 7.5.
- **2-0**: Book's record against Nate in title games
- **207**: Book's Week 7 score, the second-highest single week in league history adjusted for era
- **2.48**: Mlekus' draft class index, the best in league history, for third place
- **15**: points from Jamaal Charles, the No. 5 overall pick
