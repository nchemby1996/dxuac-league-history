# Six and Seven and Champion: Josh Book Does It Again From the 8 Seed

For thirteen weeks, 2022 belonged to John Barrows. His Hall Of Shame scored 2,208.5 points, a 118.9 scoring index that still ranks as the second-best season in league history, with four weekly high scores and a .762 all-play mark. He was 2-3 after five weeks, then won seven of his last eight, closing on six straight to lock up the No. 1 seed at 9-4. Ethan Hess had the flashier start: ChubbHub CH opened 7-0, the back half of a nine-game winning streak that began in 2021, then went 1-5 the rest of the way and slid to the 4 seed. Jesse Christman's Bear Man took the 2 seed at 9-4 on a 101.8 index, with 2.41 more wins than his all-play record called for. James Brooks's Rub'n'Tug Rapscallions grabbed the 3 seed.

Josh Book looked like a team going nowhere. Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie started 4-1, went 2-6 from there, lost the last three weeks of the season and limped in at 6-7: the 8 seed, a 94.5 scoring index and not a single weekly high score. But Book was busy in the trade market, and two of the season's nine trades were his. In week 8 he sent Deebo Samuel to Christman for Chris Olave and David Montgomery. In week 9 he swung a six-player deal with Barrows, giving up Stefon Diggs, T.J. Hockenson and Nyheim Hines for Jaylen Waddle, Zach Ertz and Kenneth Walker III. That was the same Kenneth Walker III Barrows had claimed in week 6 for $601, the season's biggest FAAB bid. Drew Martin was the league's busiest dealer, a party to six of the nine trades.

The bracket made Barrows pay for that deal. Book beat Tyler Harris 157-120 in the first round, then drew the top seed in the quarterfinals and beat him 188-130.5, the biggest winners-bracket score of the postseason. Josh Allen put up 44, Waddle 25 and Walker 11 against his old team, while Diggs managed 11 and Hockenson 9 for Barrows. The No. 1 seed finished eighth. The quarterfinals also produced a dead heat: Brooks and Nate Hemby both scored exactly 154, and Brooks, the higher seed, moved on. Christman beat Kyle Loy 163-128.5 and Hess outlasted Tanner Edwards 173.5-151.5. In the semifinals Book handled Hess 157.5-114.5 and Brooks knocked out Christman 147-131.5.

The title game was ugly, and Book won it anyway, 111.5-93.5. He got nothing from quarterback Josh Allen or tight end Dawson Knox, both in his lineup in the week the Bills' game in Cincinnati was never completed. The Lions Coach slot carried him with 27.5, and Brooks couldn't take advantage: Justin Jefferson gave him 3 points. It was Book's seventh championship and his third back-to-back, after 2011-12 and 2014-15. No other owner has repeated even once. He also became the first 8 seed to win the league, and his 94.5 index is the second-lowest of any champion, ahead of only Barrows in 2020.

Draft day belonged to Hess. His class graded out as the best in the league (2.13 class index, third-best ever), led by Patrick Mahomes at pick 20, the most valuable pick of the draft at +214 points over replacement. The top of the board was rough. Drew Martin took Jonathan Taylor first overall and got -13 VOR, and his second-rounder, Javonte Williams, scored 38 points for -119. Brooks's class ranked dead last at -1.20, yet Jefferson at No. 2 (+207) helped carry him to the final.

At the bottom, Tanner Forrest's Gettin' Higgy With It went 2-11 on an 83.1 index, lost 141.5-95.5 to Edwards in the first round and finished 12th.

## By the numbers

- **118.9**: John Barrows's scoring index, second-best in league history, worth an eighth-place finish.
- **8**: Josh Book's seed, the lowest ever to win the title until the next season.
- **154-154**: Brooks vs. Hemby in the quarterfinals; Brooks, the 3 seed, advanced.
- **$601**: Barrows's FAAB bid for Kenneth Walker III, who scored 41 points for Book in the last three rounds.
- **7**: Josh Book's championships, from 8 title-game trips.
