# Lucky Number Seven, Seeded Eight

Every few years the league convinces itself that Josh Book is finally done. In 2022 it had a very good case.

Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie started 4-1, then fell apart. Book went 2-6 the rest of the way, lost his last three games and crawled into the playoffs at 6-7 as the 8 seed, without a single weekly high score all year. The defending champion looked like a guy saying goodbye.

The team everyone was actually afraid of was John Barrows. Hall Of Shame scored 2,208.5 points, a 118.9 scoring index that still ranks as the second-best season in league history. Barrows went 2-3 out of the gate, then won seven of his last eight and took the No. 1 seed. Ethan Hess, meanwhile, ran out to a 7-0 start, then went 1-5 and slid to the 4 seed. Hess had also drafted better than anyone, landing Patrick Mahomes with pick 20, the most valuable pick of the draft. It bought him a fast start and a bad memory.

The season turned in week 9, and nobody noticed. Book and Barrows swung a six-player deal: Stefon Diggs, T.J. Hockenson and Nyheim Hines went to Barrows, and Jaylen Waddle, Zach Ertz and Kenneth Walker III went to Book. Barrows had won Walker in week 6 with a $601 bid, the biggest of the season. He spent that money, it turned out, on a gift for Josh Book.

Book beat Tyler Harris in the first round. Then the bracket served up the top seed, and the trade came due. Book dropped 188 on Barrows, the biggest winners-bracket score of the postseason, and Waddle and Walker both scored against their old boss. The best scoring team in the league finished eighth. Hall Of Shame, indeed.

The quarterfinals had one more cruelty. James Brooks and Nate Hemby both scored exactly 154. Brooks, the higher seed, advanced. Hemby went home from a game he didn't lose, which is a very Moosey Moosey Moosey way to leave a postseason.

Book then handled Hess in the semifinal and met Brooks in the final, where neither team seemed to want it. Book got nothing from Josh Allen or Dawson Knox and won anyway, 111.5-93.5, carried by 27.5 points from his Lions Coach slot. Brooks got 3 points from Justin Jefferson, the second overall pick, and that was that.

It was Book's seventh championship. Nobody else has more than two. It was his third back-to-back, and no other owner has repeated even once. He became the first 8 seed to win the league, and his 94.5 scoring index is the second-lowest of any champion. Book didn't need the best team. He needed Barrows's running back.

At the bottom sat Tanner Forrest, and he got there fast. One year after losing the title game to Book, Gettin' Higgy With It kept the name and lost the plot: 2-11, an 83.1 index, a 141.5-95.5 first-round loss to Tanner Edwards and a 12th-place finish. Same team name, very different team.

## By the numbers

- **118.9**: Barrows's scoring index, second-best in league history. The prize was eighth place.
- **$601**: what Barrows paid for Kenneth Walker III, who then helped knock him out.
- **154-154**: Brooks vs. Hemby. Hemby didn't lose, and he still went home.
- **2-11**: Tanner Forrest's record, one season after playing for the title.
- **7**: Josh Book's championships. Nobody else has more than two.
