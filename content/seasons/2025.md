# Burrow, Borrowed Back: Barrows Beats Forrest in a Title Game Again

The league trimmed its benches, added a second flex, dropped the head-coach slot and started scoring to the hundredth of a point. It took Luke Martin four weeks to make the decimals matter. Country Roads Take Mahomes beat James Brooks 144.5-144.48, a 0.02-point win, the narrowest in league history.

That was the closest anyone came to stopping him. Luke lost once, to Drew Martin in week 5, and never again. He finished 12-1, the best regular-season record the league has ever seen, and carries an eight-game winning streak into next season. The defending champion had become a machine.

Machines, it turns out, are vulnerable to 4-9 teams. Tyler Harris backed into the playoffs as the 9 seed, beat Nate Hemby 159.26-79.2 in the first round, then met the 12-1 top seed. ESPN shows Harris starting only 10 players in an 11-man lineup. He won anyway, 117.40-113.86. Luke Martin has now been the No. 1 seed three times, including an 11-1 year in 2020, and has never won a title from the top spot. He won his last ring from the 7 seed. From the 1 seed he gets a nice plaque.

At the other end of the standings, the league's greatest champion turned into its worst team. Three years after his seventh title, Josh Book went 1-12, tying Jesse Christman's 2009 for the worst record in league history. His one win came against Kyle Loy in week 6. He allowed more points than anyone, his luck score was the second-worst ever, and he lost his last seven games, a streak that also carries into next season. Even a 1-12 team gets a playoff game in this league. Book lost that too.

Ethan Hess had his own record to set. Aaron Hernandez's Taxi Service posted a 75.2 scoring index, the lowest in league history. Hess also drafted Drake Maye in round 15 and dropped him in week 2. Tanner Forrest picked him up off the street, and Maye became the best late-round value of the draft.

Forrest and Christman ran the busiest trade market on record, 16 deals in all, each of them in eight. Forrest traded his No. 1 overall pick, Ja'Marr Chase, to Christman in week 6 and got him back in week 12. He also pried Puka Nacua away from Loy for Harold Fannin Jr. In the semifinal, Nacua scored 52.5 as DEAN dropped 243.7 on Harris. Forrest had built a monster. He was going back to the final.

So was John Barrows, who had been waiting on him there before. Barrows made one trade all year, getting Jaxon Smith-Njigba from Drew Martin, and one move that mattered more. He had claimed Joe Burrow for $19 in week 10, cut him in semifinal week, then spent $525 to claim him back for the final. Burrow scored 26.4. Bijan Robinson, the No. 2 overall pick, scored 45.9. Maye gave Forrest 42.44, and it wasn't close: Barrows won 177.1-143.24.

Barrows has two titles, 2020 and 2025, and both came against Tanner Forrest. Forrest is 0-5 in championship games. He keeps building the house. Someone else keeps moving in.

The commissioner, for the record, was the 8 seed, scored 79.2 in his first-round loss and finished 12th.

## By the numbers

- **12-1**: Luke Martin's record, the best ever. It got him one playoff game.
- **0.02**: Luke Martin's margin over James Brooks in week 4, the narrowest win in league history.
- **1-12**: Josh Book's record, tying Jesse Christman's 2009 for the worst ever.
- **75.2**: Ethan Hess's scoring index, the lowest the league has seen.
- **$19 and $525**: what Barrows paid for Joe Burrow the first time and the second time.
- **0-5**: Tanner Forrest in title games.
