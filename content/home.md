# Nineteen Years, One Josh Book

In 2007, eight guys started an ESPN league and called it the D.J.U.A.C. Josh Book, Nate Hemby, Tyler Harris, John Barrows, Kyle Loy, Curtiss Hemby, Ryan Hemby and Dylan Plouvier drafted offline, started a punter and a head coach, and played a scoring system so generous that Nate put up 10,759.5 points in one regular season. Nobody knew what a reasonable fantasy score looked like. Nobody cared. The first championship game was decided by seven and a half points, Book over Nate, 797.5-790, and the rest of the league has spent every year since trying to get that trophy back from him.

It has mostly not gone well.

## The early chaos

The first few years were a league still figuring out what it was. The roster changed almost every season: eight teams, then 11, then eight, then ten. The punter was gone after one year. PPR left in 2008 and came back in 2010. Owners arrived for a season and vanished. Tyler Harris won the second title as *T Doggie*, then spent 2009 as *tdogg beast* leading the league in points and losing the final to Nate, whose *Mr.Dr.Professor Nay Nay* finally cashed a ring two years after losing the first one. In 2010 Seth Chandler stole one as *Team sethinator*, a No. 3 seed who would never reach another final.

Then Josh Book remembered he was Josh Book.

## The dynasty

From 2011 to 2015, *Joshy Woshy Puddin' N' Pie* won four titles in five years. He won as a 3 seed. He won as a 6 seed while giving up the most points in the league. He won as the No. 1 seed, twice. The only interruption was 2013, when Luke Martin's *Maryland Terps* took the title and briefly made it look like there would be a rivalry. Luke also lost title games to Book in 2012 and 2014, so it was less a rivalry than a hostage situation.

By the middle of the decade the league had settled into its modern shape: ten teams, a snake draft from 2015, a flex spot and a head coach slot that refused to die. Jesse Christman crashed the party in 2016 as *Password Is Taco*, a 6 seed. Drew Martin's *The Fumbling Flounders* won it as the top seed in 2017, beating Book in the final. Nate added his second as *Moosey Moosey Moosey* in 2018. Christman named his 2019 team *2X Champ* and then made it true.

## The modern league

FAAB showed up in 2019 with a $1,000 budget, and the waiver wire stopped being a race to the refresh button. In 2021 the league grew to 12 teams, brought in James Brooks and Tanner Edwards, and stretched the regular season to 13 weeks. Book responded by winning two more titles, in 2021 and 2022, the second one as an 8 seed. No 8 seed had ever won it before. Then Tanner Edwards did it again the very next year with *Kitties and Samsquanches*.

That is the strange thing about this league's playoffs: the seeding barely matters. The No. 1 seed has won just 4 of 19 titles. From 2022 to 2024, the champion was seeded seventh or lower three years in a row, capped by Luke Martin's *Chig Chig Time* winning as a 7 seed in 2024, eleven years after his first. John Barrows won *Mediocre at Best* in 2020 and *Deshaun's RubnTizzuggers* in 2025, both times from the middle of the bracket.

## The people

Five of the original eight are still here. Book, Nate and Tyler have played all 19 seasons. Barrows has played 18, taking 2010 off. Kyle Loy has played 14 and holds the best single week in league history once you adjust for scoring era: 255-145 over Barrows in 2020.

Twenty-five owners have come through in total, and the league has produced its share of characters. Drew Martin has been involved in 22 of the league's 47 trades since 2018. Tanner Forrest is 0-5 in championship games, the most finals without a title in league history. Tyler Harris has gone 17 seasons since his only title, the longest active drought. Brooks has the best win percentage in league history among owners with five or more seasons, and no ring. And Nate holds the league's longest winning streak, 15 straight across 2010 and 2011, which he will bring up whether or not anyone asks.

## The scoreboard

Seven titles for Josh Book. Two each for Luke Martin, Jesse Christman, Nate Hemby and John Barrows. One each for Tyler Harris, Seth Chandler, Drew Martin and Tanner Edwards. Over 1,532 games, the league has seen a 0.02-point loss, a 143-0 final and more terrible team names than this site can possibly do justice to.

Every one of those games is in the archive. Pick a season, pick an owner, pick a grudge. Somebody in this league is still mad about it.
